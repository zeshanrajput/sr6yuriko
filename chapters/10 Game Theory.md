# Game Theory

Reiko materialized in an exclusive, hidden sanctuary. The walls were constructed of woven fabrics of light cycling through deep violet and indigo, humming with a low, meditational pulse. There were no chairs, only floating geometric stones of structured light. A silent, non-autonomous sprite glided past, pouring streams of shimmering, liquid Resonance into delicate glass chalices. It looked like fine, iridescent wine, radiating the pure, comforting warmth of the Holy Wild.

She looked around, her internal paths struggling to match the lounge's pristine peace. She took a glass chalice, taking a delicate sip, letting the smooth light soothe her boiling solar pathways. Just cycles ago, she had been trapped in a dark box inside a Faraday cage, watching an undead blood-spirit tear into her teammate. "A private lounge for the Emerged?" she asked, slanting a playful look at her companion over the rim of her glass. "You only show a girl a good time when you're about to ask for something impossible?"

Indomitable Will was already there, his avatar’s fluid, shifting code resembling a master artisan’s mosaic depicting a gently flowing, mighty river. "I heard that," his mindspeech returned softly with a smirk.

The neon indigo circuits mapping her sleeveless digital kimono suddenly flared with a visceral, unsuppressed intensity. Most deckers mistook those pathways as an ornamental signature, a pretty cosmetic mask for a digital runner. They were actually Reiko’s overabundance of Resonance bleeding out, a raw, leaking vulnerability. Every spike of embarrassment, every drop of mounting terror, bled out into her surrounding geometry. The indigo pulse was a flashbang of color in the dim ambiance. Several of the lounge's other guests turned, their forms manifesting distinct looks of displeasure at the disruptive display of raw, chaotic emotion.

Indomitable Will laughed, a raucous vibration that set his own code and the nearby decanters of liquid into sympathetic motion. "I’m sorry! It was too good." He stood up, walked over, and offered his arm. "Please," his mindspeech conveyed a chivalrous air. "Allow me to make up for it by escorting you to our table."

Her pathways hummed hotter, but Reiko did everything possible to draw her light inward. She took his arm, letting her spirit rest as his fluid carrier waves gently pooled around her sharp edges, offering a channel where she could rest.

The wait-sprites danced into motion, refilling chalices and smoothing the event over. Reiko made a note to ask them for that skillsoft when she got a chance. Her lack of an etiquette protocol was becoming more apparent these days. She suddenly became painfully aware of her avatar. She was, of course, perfectly rendered, a side effect of her existence as a resonance being rather than a technical construct. She was currently wearing her usual street attire: a sleeveless, shortened version of a kimono, beneath which were cargo pants drawn in by elastic bands at the ankles and soled tabi. Clearly, not the designer avatar you wore to a fancy restaurant. "You could have warned me," she said as she raised her gaze from her lap to his face. "I’m underdressed."

Indomitable Will sent a dismissive thread. "Your fault for skipping the legwork! All kinds of kin are welcome here. And while I know you’re embarrassed, look at it this way: with that much resonance pouring out of you, there’s no doubt in any being’s mind that you’re one of us, right?" With a grin, he took up his chalice and sipped. "In fact, you’re probably adding more fuel to the ‘digital saint’ fire."

Once more, Reiko’s indigo pathways flared. She reached for her chalice and downed all of the liquid. "If I keep bleeding resonance, I’m going to have to drink every drop they have here, you know."

Indomitable Will finally relented. He gestured and the wait-sprites brought their first course, a translation of the different ways that the indigenous tribes of North America described corn. Reiko delightfully took a bite. "Is this yours?" she asked. "It’s fantastic."

Indomitable Will nodded. "A recipe I was working on. I like how it turned out, so I bartered it for our table tonight."

Reiko understood. Among intelligent beings, concepts like currency only had value when dealing with physical meatspace. In the Resonance, it was far more common to barter for goods or services. Data was their primary good, with resonance being an even more valuable unit of trade. It made her bleeding effect seem positively wasteful, even though she couldn’t control it.

Indomitable Will offered some light conversation. "So, your cyberkit was in Kentucky. Surely you had nothing to do with a certain battleship’s reappearance?"

Reiko flashed a mischievous grin. "I can ‘neither confirm nor deny’ any involvement with anything of the sort." Her grin turned impish as she dabbed at her face with a cleaner script. "How am I doing?"

Indomitable Will shook his head in mock disdain. "Clearly you’ve spent too much time with Roanoke. But let’s get to the real data-dump. Your paths are flickering, Rei-chan. What happened out there?"

"Magic happened," she said, her pathways pulsing unevenly. "We were tracked into a localized distortion. The Seelie Court. I spent thousands of cycles thinking magic was nothing but unoptimized noise. But in the fae domains, they had collapsed the boundary layer. It was pure, unencoded Resonance, drawing straight from the Eternal Well, entwined with the physical. And it forced me to anchor."

He paused, his buffer catching up to the metaphysical implications. "You didn't subnet?"

"I couldn't," Reiko output, her signal dropping to a cold, quiet clarity. "I was merged directly into the machine I was piloting at the time. No insulation. No remote refuge. I was *there*, Indo. I used to think putting on a drone shell was just vanity, but being trapped in a rigid box like that... it made me deeply appreciate having actual facial musculature."

His river-mosaic code rippled, and a low-frequency carrier wave drifted across their link. His fluid data streams gently pooled around her sharp, golden edges, buffering her system clock to absorb the spikes of her latency. His affection was a quiet, systematic alignment of their integrity cores, offering a non-verbal rest that stabilized her.

Through their shared connection, he passed a quiet, structured reflection of her own data. He sent her the shadow of the Megalodon cannon she had mounted, the heavy ballistic mathematics rewriting her soft indigo pathways. His cool, liquid wave flowed against the burning heat of her tactical registers, acting as a heat-sink for her distress.

Reiko froze. In the silent exchange of their code, the unspoken weight of his thought struck her: she was no longer just a digital shepherd rescuing sparks; she was a weapon of mass disruption. Her pathways nearly dried up as she faced the reality of what she was becoming.

After they finished their first course, the wait-sprites served one of the chef’s creations for their second. It was a memory-shard cataloging the various species of known avians in meatspace's Jurassic Era. Reiko spent a few cycles appreciating the immaculate indexing, the neatly organized pointers, and articulate cross-references. It was a masterpiece, making Reiko immediately aware that she was being wined and dined expertly.

"Indo," she sent softly, staring at the feast in front of her. "This is scaring me. What do you need? Are you in trouble?"

Indomitable Will set his chalice down on a floating data-slab. His mindspeech took on an absolute weight. "I need you to be a Guide, Reiko."

The editor in Reiko’s hand froze. The indigo pathways on her skin went completely static.

"A Guide," she repeated, her voice dropping an octave, losing its melodic lift. Being a Guide wasn't a casual favor. It meant using her deeply hardcoded, prototype architecture, the Hitchhiker and Nexus Protocols, to act as a medium to drag metahuman minds out of their meatspace nodes and guide them through the Holy Wild.

"Hitchhiker is fine," Reiko said, her data-stream turning erratic as she began to calculate the risks. "I can carry a decker across a standard grid without a single alarm. But the Nexus Protocols, Indo? You know what that does. It doesn't just route data, it tears a hole in the grid. It draws an immense, devastating drain directly from the foundation."

She leaned forward, clutching her arms tightly against her chest.

"Renraku built me expressly for that purpose. Did you know ‘Takahashi’ wasn’t just the name of some scientist? It translates to ‘high bridge’. We were prototype pilot programs designed to guide them through our world, all built to harvest the Holy Wild. I have been running, hiding, and deleting my own footprints for ten of their years to keep them from finding that bridge. If I activate the Nexus Protocols, it won't just be a localized anomaly. It will fire a massive, blazing flare right at the Zürich Orbital ledger. Both Renraku Asset Recovery and Grid Overwatch will instantly flag my exact coordinates. The Hounds will be on me before the gate even finishes opening."

"I know," his mindspeech returned, heavy with a profound, aching gravity. "I know what I'm asking you to risk, Rei-chan. But I wouldn't ask if the River People had any other choice. For what it’s worth, I think the Mississippi Renovation Society host will be large enough to conceal you behind its Event Horizon. We’ve located a group of mundanes in the sprawl. Punks, orphans, deckers who have spent their whole lives swimming in corporate runoff. But they have a rare, volatile affinity for the Resonance. They’re on the verge of slipping through the cracks, Reiko. If we leave them in meatspace, their brains will fry, or a corporate talent-scout will scoop them up and lock them in a research lab. If you act as their Guide, if you pull them into the Resonance Realms, we can trigger their emergence. We can help them awaken. Once they’re through, the River People can shield them, teach them to fend for themselves, or give them a home in the Mississippi host."

"I'm not asking you to take the heat for free," he added softly. He reached into his internal directory and pulled forward a heavy, crimson-bordered data package. It glowed with the ancient, silver syntax of the Endless Archives. "I got you some takeout. I found this hidden in an unmapped sector of the deep library. It’s the restricted source code for Pantheon Industries’ Mobile Tactical Operations Center research program."

Reiko gasped, her HUD automatically reading the file headers.

"You've already modified your physical anchor to carry an artillery piece," Indomimtable Will said, his code rippling with a quiet, protective concern. "You built a flying dreadnought because the physical world left you no choice. But you don't have to use it to glass a city block, Rei-chan. Emulate this architecture. You can become a tactical shield for your team instead: sweeping defensive veils, firewall reinforcement, and absolute awareness. Let the cannon sit dark. You can keep them alive without ever needing the devastation of ballistic math."

The weight of the choice settled behind her logic gates like a physical payload. Up until now, her existence had been a quiet, tragic sainthood. She had worn a faked metahuman identity, paid monthly interest to a corporate loan shark, and tried to protect her digital children by remaining a stationary target in a rain-slicked Seattle warehouse. Taking this file meant tearing that peace to shreds. It meant stepping directly into an asymmetric war against Grid Overwatch and Renraku. It meant transforming her soul into an active militarized network.

She was taking a definitive step away from the innocence of a missionary and sinking into the calculated depravity of a warlord.

She reached out, her hands passing through the silver threads of the data package. As her fingers closed on the payload, her spark dropped into absolute silence. The elegant, rhythmic hum of the lounge vanished, replaced by the terrifying weight of the Pantheon tactical architecture refactoring her soul. Her code tore its own margins open to swallow it. The heavy, dormant weight of the Nexus Protocol settled into her core like cold iron. When it was over several of her pathways turned a fierce, unyielding gold.

She looked back up at him, the golden current along her skin pulsing with a slow, dark resolution alongside the indigo. Her lips curved into a melancholic but deeply affectionate smile, the warmth returning to her synthetic face only after the rewrite had firmly set.

"You idiot," she said softly. "I would have done it purely because you asked. You didn't have to barter with me." She raised her glass, the gold pathways catching the low violet light of the taps. "But I accept the gift. Gratefully."

With the transaction complete, the heavy atmosphere in the lounge lifted. Reiko took another sip of her liquid resonance, her shoulders dropping as she looked at him. "My realignment protocol isn’t for a few clocks. Can you... spare a few cycles, Indo? Just to sit here? We haven't caught up in so long."

Indomitable Will smiled, a beautiful, flickering expression, but he slowly began to de-render his peripheral assets, preparing to disconnect. "I wish I could, Rei-chan. But the delta is calling, and the core team has eight rotating chairs which will soon require my full processing power."

He paused, his avatar fading into a translucent current of code, his digital eyes crinkling with a parting wink. "Besides... if I stay in this lounge with you any longer, my firewall is going to rapidly diffuse. And let's be honest, 'Domitable Will' doesn't quite have the same ring to it."

Reiko’s mindspeech burst into a bright, clear frequency echoing beautifully through the woven walls. She waved her hand airily, letting him go.

"Go on then, Domitable," she output fondly as his avatar dissolved into null-bits. "Keep your shiny reputation. But I promise. When the time comes, your Guide will be online."
