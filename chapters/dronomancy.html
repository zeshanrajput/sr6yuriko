A Drone in Every Port

A DI-to-DI guide on having drones around the world

by: Yuriko Star

Hi hi! Yuriko here, your favorite Shiawase Desert Wars correspondent! Today I’ll be answering a question from one of our viewers!

Dear Yuriko! Jumping around from body to body seems convenient. How do you do it? Any tips?

Yes! Why pay for commercial airfare when you can get there instantly? It saves a ton of time, a ton of nuyen, and helps you get things done the world over. Let’s set ourselves up for success:

  • The Real Deal on Having This Many Drones
  • How to Connect
  • The Mechanics of Control
  • What to Do When Things Go Wrong

One thing - yes, I’m focused on anthroform drones here, but almost everything applies to other drones as well. Though good luck trying to ship your Shadowdancer freight. And why do you even have one of those?!? Do you even know what ‘subtle’ means?

The Real Deal on Having This Many Drones

Well. It’s no secret that I really, really, really like Shiawase drones. And seriously, no other manufacturer can get my facial muscles right. But why so many? The answer is simple: it’s an investment in your future! Having drones in multiple locations means you can get to work instantly, no matter where the job takes you.

Think of it like this: each drone is like a little version of you, ready to spring into action at a moment’s notice. And the more drones you have, the more “you” there are in the world, ready to tackle any challenge that comes your way!

Most importantly, having your own drone in geographically diverse locations means you can modify them to your liking. Let’s be real - the stock Butler face isn’t fooling anyone. And shout out to all you casemodders out there - I know you like jamming every ounce of capability into those mechanized monstrosities! You do you!

TipGame Mechanics

The alternative to owning a drone at each location is to rent a drone. In Lifestyles of the Shadowy and Infamous (p. 19) there’s a section on vehicle rentals. This costs 4 lifestyle points and normally lets you rent a vehicle. Drone rental isn’t specifically discussed, and if you want a licensed or illegal drone, that’s probably not doable. It’s an option you could discuss with a GM, but for SRM it’s not really on the table.

Tips and Tricks on Saving That Nuyen

  1. Be a shill. No seriously - you’re potentially talking about several drones that are the same manufacturer, if not configuration. Work a deal! You might save up to 10% on your original purchase costs! If you use an official Shiawase distributor, you can use my promo code ‘Yuri-chan is amazing!!’ to get an additional discount! (The code has limited uses, so act fast!)
TipGame Mechanics

There’s a quality called ‘Smile for the Camera!’ in Para Bellum (pg. 18), the first Desert Wars supplement. It gives you up to 10% off the cost of purchases from a single manufacturer.

And if you’re ever playing a mission with this character and need help buying a drone, let’s talk. =)

  1. Find your drones yourself! I know, it’s super convenient to ‘know a guy’, but the middleman almost always comes with a markup. We’re all experts in using the Matrix, so do the legwork yourself! Don’t forget some simple programs like a Shopsoft can really help you snag a deal.
TipGame Mechanics

The Shadowrun Missions Guide’s Downtime section (in v2.4, it’s pg.18) covers purchasing gear in depth. The short version: build up your Matrix Search Pool, run Overclock and a Shopsoft. You may need to spend downtime actions to buy some additional dice for a hard to find item.

That being said, with enough favor points built up it’s theoretically possible that a contact can get you the item you want at or below list price. The rules are in the SRMG in the same place.

  1. Elbow grease! I know, it’s not always the most fun option, but modifying your drones yourself is the single biggest tip I have for all you casemodding fanatics. Ob-vi, you’re going to need hands to work on a drone and some tools - this might be a time to rent a cheap drone locally if you don’t have two in one location.
TipGame Mechanics

The rules for modifying a vehicle or drone yourself are in Double Clutch (p. 120). You’re going to need to do some Matrix searching to find the right components, so don’t neglect to build up your Matrix Search Pool. In SRMG modifying a single drone is a downtime action (v2.4, p. 19), but doing so lets you save 50% off the cost of mods other than accessories.

You’ll also need to buy hits for the modification test, because this is done during downtime. Even if you have enough downtime during a mission to do a mod, modifications are permanent changes to your character (SRMG v2.4, p. 17) so you have to buy hits regardless. If you have the Pilot Origins quality or another way of running autosofts, there is an Engineering autosoft available.

  1. Keep your cost per drone down. I know, this sucks, but seriously - you might have 3 or more of these things floating around, and they add up. The some important things for a DI (just my opinion):
  • Sensor Upgrades. Seriously, these little babies are your window to the world. Don’t skimp here.
NoteGame Mechanics

What we’re worried about here comes from Hack and Slash (p. 123) - “if the device’s rating is less than your Data Processing, then your opponent gains an Edge for each action you take against it”. In SR6B (p. 201), for these purposes we can consider the drone’s Device Rating to be equal to their Sensor Rating. And Hack and Slash (p.124) says when we optimize a drone, one of the benefits is that we can consider the Device Rating as four higher for the purposes of determining edge use against a DI.

What this all adds up is that you should have a Sensor Rating of at least your Data Processing minus 4. Don’t forget to consider any temporary bonuses you get to Data Processing, like the Toolbox program.

  • Retractable Skates. Drones are slow. For any drone with cyberlimbs, these will cost you a list price of 250 nuyen per foot and will make sure you don’t get outrun by that bookworm hermetic who hasn’t done leg day in twenty years. Super embarassing.
TipGame Mechanics

The retractable skates are in Body Shop (p. 47). These do not change your drone’s attributes but give you a modified movement rate of 10/30/+2. They take a minor action to deploy or retract, but when operated wirelessly the minor action is refunded. You’ll also appear to be running around barefoot, so think carefully about your footware choices.

Double Clutch (p. 146) adds a full set of cyberlimbs to the anthroform drones in the core rulebook. All the anthroform drones in Double Clutch already have a full set of cyberlimbs. And remember - as a DI you don’t have essence, so you can go all out on the cyberware!

  • Realistic Features. There are a ton of places that drones aren’t welcome. Don’t be left out in the cold. Shiawase offers the entry package stock on all Butlers! Just… put some armor on if you’re going to try and use a Butler in a firefight… but I do, so hey.
TipGame Mechanics

Double Clutch (p. 146) adds realistic features to the MCT Gnat (4), Shiawase Inu (2, 3 for an additional 500 nuyen) and Hornet (1). The notable anthroform drone to come with Realistic Features is, of course, the Butler (Double Clutch, p. 117).

  • Software Tags. Yes, we can all emulate whatever we need but you really don’t want to run your Fork off of that public router, do you? And software tags are really inexpensive. Don’t be a cheapskate.

  • Hydrocarbon Fuel Converter. Shoutout to Line for this one! Yes, your drone can draw energy from the Grid while you’re in range, but you may not always be in range and six hours just isn’t going to cut it. Bonus - you can connect this to your oropharynx and be able to mimic eating with the meatpuppets!

  • Regular armor. This one is more important for the Butler, but technically any anthroform drone can wear armor. Which is nice because without it, well, let’s just say that drones are flimsy.

TipGame Mechanics

The SRMG (v2.4 p. 27) specifically allows an anthroform drone to wear armor. You will use the higher rating of either the drone’s armor or the worn armor. Certain armors have the cumulative property, but it’s a subject of debate how many of these truly stack. My take is that it’s better to wear armor for unarmored or lightly armored drones, but to increase the drone’s armor rating for heavily armored drones.

Also on armor, the SkinShield (Firing Squad, p. 29) is a pretty reasonable way to get RACS 2 (Firing Squad, p. 54) for a moderate price on an otherwise unarmored drone. But don’t wear anything over it, or the RACS will be worthless to you. It also has a concealable holster, but I have no idea how that works given that this is a skinsuit.

Moving Your Bodies Around

  1. Ship freight! It’s cheap, it’s easy, and no one will bother you. And we’re all DIs anyway, so you can go hang out at Sector 213 or your favorite digi-bar while your puppet takes the slow boat to Seattle. Or log into another one, you workaholic you. Oh! And if you stop in at 213, tell them Yuri-chan sent you.
  2. Set your drone to power-up when it arrives. It’s a PITA when your drone arrives and you can’t access it. Trust me.
  3. Pack your drone so that you can get out of the box. It’s SUPER embarassing when your limbs are all disconnected and you can’t even get out of the box. Don’t ask me how I know.

How to Connect

Okay, your body has arrived at your target location and you’re ready to occupy it.

Question 1 Operating a drone remotely versus directly inhabiting it or a local device.

  • Operating a drone remotely means your traffic will be routed from your home device to the drone.

    • If you do this via the Grid it’s fine, and you will have no noise penalties. But this leaves a trail back to your home device if anyone is looking. {Game Mechanics: Hack and Slash p. 49 states “using a global host link to avoid noise earns a Matrix User +20 OS per connection” for illegal actions.}
    • If you do this via your own hardware without boosters, you’re limited by distance. Up to about a kilometer you’ll likely be fine, but it gets dicey up to 100km and pretty much impossible without that (SR6B p.177).
    • If you use boosters, there’s two ways I can think of. You’ll likely want to equip your drones with satellite links (500 nuyen, SR6B p. 268) regardless.
      • Try and scrub the noise. Using programs like Signal Scrubber, ECM Warrior II (M-TOCs only) and so on can help. You can also try to use matrix relays (Hack and Slash p. 42). And RCCs include built-in noise cancellation {Game Mechanics: An RCC reduces noise by its device rating (SR6B p. 197).} You’ll want at least 5 points of noise reduction for the satellite link alone, plus a buffer in case you’re attacked by noise generation.
      • Retransmit your signal using a Retrans unit. {Game Mechanics: These are in Double Clutch (p. 141) and rebroadcast a specific signal, eliminating all noise on the signal up to that point. You would want one of these at each remote drone location, and they must be mounted on a vehicle or drone.}
    • Of course, anytime you operate a vehicle or drone remotely you’re subject to attacks by noise generators. If they overrun your ability to handle noise, you’ll be cut off from your body.

  • Operating a drone locally means you need to move to it.

    • This is perfectly legal, so using the Grid means you can get a clean connection and not worry about half of you getting lost when a flaky router just drops some packets.
    • A related option is to operate the drone locally and have a remote connection to your expensive assets (M-TOCs, cyberdecks, etc.). This is what I personally do. That way, if I suffer a noise attack I can’t mitigate I only lose connection to my remote resources.

    TIP: No matter what, always have an escape route! For us, this means a connection back to the Matrix in case the drone is lost. I can’t count how many faraday cages, null zones, and the like I’ve had to operate in.

    • Laser Jacks (Hack and Slash p. 41) can get a basic connection going between two devices with line of sight, up to about 2 kilometers on a clear day. These won’t work for operating your drone remotely unless you use AR commands (I do most of the time) because they don’t have enough bandwidth. But for an emergency escape, they’ll work. I have some mounted on my gnats and bring a drill to poke holes in walls.
    • Cable is fantastic and is only limited by how much you can carry. Good quality costs you about a nuyen per meter (Hack and Slash p. 44). I have (regrettably) had to work an undersea operation and brought a kilometer of cable, a satellite link, and a buoy. Not my finest hour.

The Mechanics of Control

Okay, you’ve got your drone in place and you’re connected. How do you want to operate your drone? This really depends on what your strengths are.

  • First things first, unless you’re jacked in your drone can (and will) be hacked (SR6B p. 197). For those of us descended from pilot programs, you know how to do this (Pilot Origins quality, Hack and Slash p. 120). I’ve heard of codemod shops that will mod your spark for a price, but I don’t truck with that. (Control Rig Integration, Hack and Slash p. 128). You use your core spark capabilities {your mental attributes, SR6B p. 197} when operating a drone this way.
  • Regardless of where and how, you need a program slot to occupy. Your drone has a small number {Pilot / 2, SR6B. p. 201}, plus whatever software tags you’ve installed. You can also shove a commlink or agent box into your drone, but this will increase your costs per drone. And if you take a moment to optimize a device, you can increase its program slots by 1 (Hack and Slash, p. 124).
  • Operating a drone via VR is probably the ‘best’ you can do if you don’t have a way to jump in directly. This lets you use your core spark capabilities {your mental attributes, see sidebar SR6B p. 180} but it’s not as easy as being jacked in.
  • Operating a drone via AR is actually better for me. Meatpuppets have to use their physical attributes {sidebar SR6B p. 180}, but I emulate this using extra data processing and sleaze algorithms {Matrix Equivalent Attributes, sidebar p. 118, Hack and Slash}. As a pilot origin AI, this is actually the most comfortable way for me to operate a drone too. {Pilot Origin quality description: “this is piloting by pure calculation and data processing…”, Hack and Slash p. 120.}
  • Autosofts:
    • There are multiple ways to learn how to use these, and I’ve already talked about a few. But remember that you have to have a different autosoft for your core piloting routines {Maneuvering and Stealth} for each drone type you operate, as well as each weapon {Targeting}. This can get super expensive!
    • I started programming my own {costs 1 karma per program, Hack and Slash p. 55} after I got the hang of them, but this takes a while {probably a downtime activity given the test interval is 8 hours, but this isn’t in the SRMG}. Access to good tools {Coding Shop or Facility, Hack and Slash p. 56} can cut the time in half!
    • If you use autosofts, don’t skimp on the rating. Respect yourself. Be good at what you do.
    • The alternative, of course, is to just learn how the meatpuppets do.

What to Do When Things Go Wrong

As DIs, we don’t exist in their reality. They have home field advantage, and anything that can go wrong will. Here’s what you do.

  1. Always have an escape route! I can’t stress this enough. Again, for us, this usually means a connection back to the Matrix in case the drone is lost. I’ve had to operate in a lot of faraday cages and null zones.
  2. If you have to operate over a distance, have a contingency plan in case you lose connection. A Personal Assistant can run in an Emulator, and can execute some basic instructions you pre-program. You could also have an Agent (AIs) or Sprite (EIs) on standby. The enemy can and will hack you. They can and will signal jam you.
  3. Fly under the radar. For a while I had an artillery cannon mounted onto one of my drones, until a friend helped me realize that’s a fantastic way to have your face plastered on the screamsheets - and become completely unemployable. The solution for us is never overwhelming firepower.
  4. If things get really bad in terms of recognition, lay low. Don’t take work for a while. Change the outward appearance on all your drones. Re-skin them. It’s a pain in the backbone, but it’s better than getting hunted down or not getting work.
