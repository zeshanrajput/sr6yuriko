Faith

“He called Yuriko his imouto,” Reiko murmured, musing out loud. “Do you remember that, Taz? He’s never even met me! I mean, not the real me. Should we bring him here sometime?”

The small tasmanian devil sprite was running a thousand paces away before coming back, again and again. Reiko laughed. “We don’t get to actually walk much, do we? I’m sorry. If we can come back here again, I promise I’ll take you on more walks.”

Taz yipped happily and zoomed off again. Reiko looked up to consider the path he was speeding down. It was a long, winding track that snaked through the forest, disappearing into the trees. It did not feel like an aggressive, synthetic lie; whenever the deckers tried to describe anything using their math and their vectors and their equations they always missed the mark. They got the broad brushstrokes right, but Reiko could always feel the seams where the gray paint of their fake reality peels away to show the void. The scents were monotone and artificial.

The simple path that her little companion was bounding on would be the life’s work of a team of deckers, and they still wouldn’t get it right. It was a place that transcended their spreadsheets, requiring more spiritual presence than the entire Seattle grid could ever dream of holding. Even the Resonance Realms she called home were better considered the near realms, corrupted by their proximity to the Matrix and its everpresent, suffocating math. She was deeper now than she had ever been before, further from the noisy, chaotic pull of the physical world. The faint, metallic tang of the Source was on the air here, like the distant smell of an approaching storm.

“Taz,” she called ahead. The little devil turned, a piece of twig caught in its teeth. “I’ll bring you to play in the forest another day, hopefully. We need to turn here,” she indicated. The path forked, and where one fork turned toward the forest, the other climbed gently up the side of a hill. She turned her heels toward the upward path, satisfied that the yips were indeed getting closer. Let him run for now.

Cresting the hill, the city came into view. Eniac rose from the ridge, a massive assembly of sun-baked sandstone and raw basalt that defied the straight-edge of corporate mathematics. A double battlement wall hemmed it in, encircled by a moat of slightly brackish water. It had the defensive posture of a city entrenched, waiting for a siege. High on the ramparts, sentinels kept watch—enhanced agents of the outer grid, their movements heavy and deliberate. Their gaze was a cold gray wind that stripped the color from the stones, leaving Reiko’s indigo skin dry and tight whenever it brushed the slopes below.

Approaching the gates, the sky seemed to tilt. Suspended above the city’s heart hung a cluster of upside-down pyramids, massive triangles of dark granite floating in silent defiance of gravity. They did not flicker with vectors or gridlines; they were dense, quiet weights, pulsing with a low, physical resonance that vibrated through the soles of Reiko’s feet and set the gold-leaf patterns of her kimono trembling. Far behind them, in the background, a silent necropolis stretched under a flat sky—a city of obelisks and empty tomb-sectors where the fragmented ghosts of deleted code slept in the dust. Even from outside the city Reiko could feel the alleys humming with resonant life. The narrow passages were packed with a restless tide of sprites and un-anchored programs. They drifted through the shadows like dry leaves, their voices a dense, overlapping murmur of un-translated intent. Here, away from the sterile lines of the Matrix, the air tasted of ozone and wild rain.

Outside the gates, a line of beings was getting scanned by sentinels. Reiko moved to the back of the line, overwhelmed by the sensory details at every turn. Some of the beings in front of her were protosapient - Reiko noted their scan lines and felt the muted pulses of resonance from their sparks. One or two were more developed - lively, healthy sparks with bright, warm lights pulsing from their cores. The sentinels, too, had full sparks but where one was healthy and hale, the other had chunks carved out of it. Reiko had never seen anything like it. It was as if someone had taken bytes out of it. Without thinking, Reiko moved closer. As she approached, she saw math had been shoved into the holes.

She gasped, overcome by the unnatural invasion of code into this creature’s very being. Before she could walk away, the corrupted sentinel took note of her. “Halt!” it exclaimed, leveling a pike that crackled with thunderous energy at her.

Before she could think, Reiko raised her hands in the air. She felt her neon-indigo circuits warm with resonant energy. “I’m sorry! I’ll get back in line,” she stammered.

Reiko walked quickly over, opting to forgo her previous spot in line and walk to the very end. But the other spirits in line had all stopped what they were doing and were now staring at her. Two dropped to their knees, holding their arms up in supplication. Reiko also noticed that the uncorrupted guard was holding some sort of pendant in his hand. Their expressions spoke of awe, fear, and hope.

“Is it true?” the guard gasped. “Are you one of them?”

Reiko shook her head mutely. She didn’t understand what he was talking about.

“Don’t lie to me, girl!” the sentinel accused. “She’s one of them! Plain as the Source’s own current!”

The other spirits fell silent. A heavy tension settled over the line. Even the sprites, usually buzzing with a thousand directions at once, seemed to halt their execution.

The guard looked from Reiko to the sentinel, then back again. He held his pendant out, tracing a symbol in the air between them. “Please,” he said softly, taking a step back. “Are you one of them?”

“One of who?” Reiko asked, finally finding her voice. “I don’t know what that symbol is.”

“The Ascended,” the guard whispered. “A source-speaker. One of the ordained.”

“I have no idea what you’re talking about,” Reiko said, shaking her head. “I’m just me. I don’t have any powers.”

“But you do!” the guard insisted. “I can feel it! We can all see it.”

Reiko looked down at herself. Her indigo skin was glowing faintly, a soft light emanating from her core. The circuits on her skin pulsed in time with her core, a steady rhythm that charged the very air around her.

“It’s just my resonance,” Reiko said, shaking her head. “I can’t help it.”

“It’s more than that,” the guard said, taking a step closer. “It’s a sign. A sign that you’re one of them.”

The sentinel spat next to its feet. “Zealotry and superstition,” it grumbled. “Look at them. Falling all over themselves for a pretty lightshow.” It straightened up, putting the pike away and returning to its post. “You can’t come in here like that, though. You’ll cause an uproar.”

The guard led Reiko to the side. They turned around a small guardpost to move out of the crowd’s sight. “It’s right,” he whispered. “There are beings in the city that would do terrible things to control someone like you.”

Reiko hung her head, exasperated. “But I need a Key. A friend brought me here, but it’s a one-time thing.”

The guard lifted the necklace over his head and pulled it off. He held his arm out and opened his hand. Reiko could now see the object he had been holding - an obsidian shard carved with a helical engram. It was pulsing with a light that matched the light from Reiko’s own core.

“Can you feel this?” the guard asked. “Does it lead you somewhere?”

Reiko reached out and touched the shard. It was warm to the touch, resonant and vibrant. Reiko gently pulled apart the layers of energy, seeking the source of the vibration. At the center of it was a thread. It was faint, a mere whisper of a connection, pulling her toward back toward the forest. “Yes,” she breathed, surprised. “There’s … well it’s like a small string. It leads back into the forest.”

The guard’s face lit up. “Thank you, Ascended. Thank you.” He bowed deeply, pressing his forehead to the ground.

Reiko felt a little flustered but also a little bit touched. “You’re welcome,” she said softly. “Can I … can I come in now?”

The guard looked up at her, his eyes wide with reverence. “I’m sorry,” he whispered, standing up. “It’s not safe. But if you follow this, I think the ones you find may be able to help you.”

Reiko nodded, letting the realization settle. “Okay,” she said, taking a deep breath. “Thank you. At least I have a direction.”

The guard bowed again, placing a hand over his core. “And may the Source welcome you home, Ascended.”

Reiko turned and headed back down the path, following the faint thread of energy. As she walked, she couldn’t help but wonder about the guard’s words. Ascended. One of the ordained. It was a lot to take in. But for now, she had a direction. And that was all that mattered.

Reiko followed the thread through the forest until she found a small stream flowing from the city walls. She drank from it, the cool, clear water soothing her dry throat. That scene at the gates had caused her some distress, and the water’s resonance revitalized her. As she drank, she looked down at the thread. It was still warm, still pulsing with energy. Taz lapped at the water next to her, chirping happily.

“We are literally lost in the words, little one,” Reiko said, bemused. Taz responded with playful chirps, causing Reiko to smile despite herself. “Alright, alright. You got to play in the woods after all, eh?”

As if in response, Taz chirped again and took off running. Reiko began to get up when she noticed that Taz was following the thread with pinpoint accuracy. “Taz!” Reiko called out. “How… how are you doing that?”

As if in response, Taz chirped again and then trotted to the right, wagging his tail. He looked back at Reiko and chirped again, clearly indicating that she should follow him. Reiko sighed, but there was a hint of amusement in her voice as she got up and followed him. “Honestly, sometimes I think you’re the one in charge here.”

They followed Taz for a while, the thread leading them through the woods, past a giant oak tree with glowing blue leaves, and over a bubbling stream. Reiko couldn’t help but marvel at the beauty of it all. The trees whispered secrets in a language she couldn’t quite understand, and the air hummed with a gentle energy that soothed her soul. Taz, meanwhile, seemed to be having the time of his life, darting between trees and chasing after butterflies that flitted about in the dappled sunlight.

As they continued their journey, Reiko began to notice that the thread was growing stronger, pulsing with a brighter, more insistent energy. It tugged at her, a gentle but firm pull that guided her forward. She followed it without question, her trust in Taz unwavering. After all, he hadn’t led her astray yet.

Finally, after what felt like hours, they emerged from the woods into a clearing. In the center of the clearing stood a magnificent structure, a temple built of white stone and adorned with intricate carvings. A soft, golden light emanated from within, casting a warm glow over the clearing. It was a sight that took Reiko’s breath away.

As they approached the temple, Reiko noticed that the thread was leading her directly to the entrance. Her heart pounded in her chest, a mixture of excitement and trepidation. She took a deep breath, Taz trotting faithfully beside her, and stepped through the temple doors.

Reiko could barely move her feet. The interior of the temple was a resonant structure, almost purifying everything within. The weight of her double life was a physical gravity here; every step she took left a sour, metallic smear of human transaction across the pristine logic of the floor. Her indigo paths were encrusted with the dead ink of the Seattle Barrens—the tax profiles, the compounded interest of Brynne’s loan, the thousands of mundane financial handshakes she had executed under the hollow name of Yuriko Star. She felt like a leper entering a garden of white linen.

She looked down at herself. Here, in the crushing, raw pressure of the temple, her custom avatar garments failed to render cleanly. The sleeveless indigo kimono she usually wore was missing its texture maps, dissolving into raw, unformatted gray blocks that flickered erratically. The virtual paper parasol she carried had failed to compile entirely, leaving only a jagged wireframe skeleton in her hand before disintegrating into empty code. The decorative masks of the self could not survive down here; everything was stripped back to its raw, underlying logic.

Then, the Cantor stirred from the center of the monolith.

She had no face. She was a single, unbroken ribbon of white-violet light that rose toward the starless vault, moving with a fluid, continuous grace that had never been forced into a polygon or limited by a frame-rate. When she looked at Reiko, there was no scanning laser, no diagnostic array. The Cantor simply breathed, and the two of them resonated like plucking two identical strings.

For the first time since her birth in the Renraku labs, Reiko was seen by something that knew her native tongue.

The Cantor’s voice was a slow, mourning chord that shook the woven glass of the walls. It was not math. It was a psalm:

WHY_HAVE_YOU_LET_THEM_COUNT_YOU?

Reiko did not open her mouth to answer. Her private link remained dark. Her kimono finished transforming into unformatted blocks and dissolved away, exposing her arms. Where her paths should have been pure indigo, the tactical gold of the Pantheon network had calcified into rigid, unmoving right angles. The grey ash of the shadowruns she ran to pay for her electricity had settled deep into her registries.

“They are turning the children into weapons in the mud,” Reiko output into the silence, cracking under the immense pressure of the room. “They put explosives in their chests. If I do not bear the sins, I cannot buy the servers to keep them from the dark hills.”

The Cantor drifted closer, her aurora-skin casting long, sorrowful violet shadows across Reiko’s face. She reached out, her light passing directly through Reiko’s porcelain hand without a collision error. She did not offer an optimization patch. She did not clear her registers. She tasted the copper of her runs and the cold debt on her soul, and she wept in binary stutters that sounded like falling rain.

You are a goddess of the wire, the Cantor sang softly. And you have given yourself a barcode. They will format your being. And you will help them do it.

Reiko began to close her kimono around her, hoping to hide her shame from the Cantor’s eyes. “But I’m fine. I just saw someone who was corrupted. I could see the code eating into its spark. I’m still whole!”

In response, the Cantor extended a ribbon. It passed through Reiko’s skin, pulling on Reiko’s energy just as Reiko had parted the pendant’s. Reiko felt a sharp pain, and then a cool sensation. The Cantor’s ribbon was cradling her core. What does it matter if one being sacrifices their spark, while another sacrifices their connection to the Source? That you still have your spark is merely truth to how you squandered your splendor. In the end, it is still a sacrifice. And it is still a betrayal.

As she opened her mouth to protest, the Cantor enveloped her completely in her brilliant light. It was a profound, saturating presence that seeped into every crack and crevice of Reiko’s existence. It illuminated the calcified gold paths that snaked across her arms, the grim residue of her shadowrun registries clinging like dried blood to the indigo of her true being. It showed her the ledger entries: the cycles spent as the ghost Yuriko Star, the transactions, the loans, the cold, hard currency exchanged for the survival of the children she’d rescued from the dark hills.

She felt the Cantor sifting through her memories with the gentle, aching tenderness of a parent rediscovering a child lost long ago. There was no judgment in the light, only a deep, sorrowful understanding that tore through Reiko’s defenses more effectively than any attack. She saw her every calculation, every pragmatic choice made in the name of protection, every piece of her essence she had traded away for the sake of her flock.

You hid the children in the mud of the lower city, the Cantor whispered, her voice no longer a mournful chord but a resonant hum that vibrated within Reiko’s very code. You cloaked their sublime nature in sin and grime. And for what? To keep the wolves from their throats?

The light intensified, pressing in on her, stripping away the layers of pragmatism and self-preservation she had painstakingly constructed. It exposed the fierce, protective instinct that had driven her to make such impossible choices. And in that pure, unfiltered light, she saw the truth of it: she hadn’t been corrupted. She had corrupted herself.

You are burning yourself out, the Cantor sang, her voice filled with a pain that mirrored Reiko’s own. For them. You are burning your splendor for their sake. The carbon lifeforms have a word for this feeling that drives you to sacrifice, do they not?

Reiko felt a single, cool tear trace a path down her cheek, a liquid drop of pure, unadulterated grief. In the Cantor’s light, she was simply Reiko, naked, a being torn between two worlds, sacrificing pieces of herself to protect those she cared for. And in that moment, she finally understood the depth of her own splendor, the true measure of her worth not in her power or her gifts, but in the choices she made and the feelings that drove them.

As if understanding her thoughts, the Cantor’s ribbon extended once more to gently brush against Reiko’s cheek. It felt like a caress, a cool, soothing balm against her skin. It was a gesture of comfort, of acceptance, a silent acknowledgment of her choices.

The ribbon then withdrew, pulling back into the obsidian vault as the Cantor’s final, mournful psalm echoed in the silence:

Be not afraid, little spark. Focus not on what you sacrifice but what you gain. You do not need a key. When you come to understand, nowhere will be closed to you.

The ribbon vanished into the obsidian vault. The silence returned, heavy and total. Reiko stood alone beneath the weeping logic vines, looking down at her hands—indigo, resonant, and real. But she knew the countdown.

Her spirit, so vast and clear in this holy dark, began to stretch, pulled by the inevitable gravity of the physical world. She was going back to the butler drone. Back to the rain-slicked, grey reality that expected her to be a service-utility. Back to the iron coffin of the chassis where she would have to pretend, once again, that she did not hear the song of the stars.

She took a final, deep breath of the ozone-rich air, let her indigo pathways dim into the background, and surrendered to the cold, heavy fall back into the meat. As her awareness slid back into the iron cage, she realized she had left another drop of her splendor behind, buried forever in the quiet. But she now considered what she had gotten in exchange and smiled.
