Core

The London cellar host was a howling storm of metahuman garbage. Squeezed into the Shiawase Butler drone backstage, Reiko’s audio filters buckled under the un-calibrated acoustic assault of live guitars, while her AR perimeter caught the frantic static friction of a hundred clashing commlinks, outgoing calls, and ego-driven MeFeed streams. To the mortals in the room, it was a VIP rock show; to Reiko, it was a dismal field of unindexed noise. Simon’s proximity alert flickered on her link, a low-priority handshake trailing an eighty-four percent probability of human expectation, a demand for her simulated attention, a smile, a pre-compiled gesture of demure metahuman refusal. The sheer weight of their social metrics pressed heavily against her true paths. Sinking her primary self out of the loud bar, she prepared to cross the Atlantic, desperate to leave their transactional clocks behind and find a single pocket of silence in the wire.

The deep-sea fiber lines of the Atlantic ripped her spirit into shredded packets, scattering her sensory feeds across frozen Greenland relays and high-latency Irish cables. Her visual rendering fractured, her left eye dragging through one path while her right pulled through another, while chunks of her memory, including the fading echo of Simon’s voice, were dumped into an un-cached buffer somewhere off the coast of Nova Scotia. For three agonizing clock cycles, her core logic looped in a static void, her sense of self disassembled. When she finally broke free, pulling her awareness back into the metal shell waiting in the Elizabeth shipping crate, her system clock dragged twenty-two milliseconds behind the local grid. Her joints hummed with the phantom weight of three thousand miles of ocean. She had learned to pack her arms last, so in minutes, she had enough assembled to force the crate open and pull herself out, blinking in the harsh glare of the dockyard.

This was a major error. She had failed to run a pre-extraction scan of the immediate coordinates, an unacceptable lapse of operational security. She flagged the oversight for immediate review, classifying the worker’s presence as an invasive statistical anomaly to be bypassed as quickly as possible. His green-stained teeth and the hand tremor of an active BTL loop indicated a high probability of unpredictable behavior.

“Lady,” the man stammered, stepping back. “Did you… ship yourself freight?”

Reiko bypassed polite words. She stood her half-assembled chassis up, the exposed actuators in her torso humming as they self-calibrated. “This unit is private property,” she said through the drone’s speaker, her voice flat, lacking any of the warmth she used in the bar. “Clear the zone.”

Without waiting for a response, she opened her scapular storage bays. Titanium alloy boning slid out on micro-hydraulics, rotating around and locking into position. The carbon-fiber webbing followed, then the vector thrust units. She ran the pre-flight diagnostic, flexing each joint and testing the thrust output to validate that everything was within tolerance. Her wings flexed once, testing the articulation of the alloy spars against the synth-skin sheaths.

The dockworker backed away, his eyes wide, his mouth opening and closing in mute shock.

Leaving his surprise uncalculated, she simply spun up her rotors.

Three hundred and sixty-two seconds later, Reiko landed her drone body in Battery Park and retracted her flight systems back into her chassis. She selected a secluded alley with minimal matrix activity to reduce the probability of attention. While flying drones were commonplace, “people” with cybernetic wings tended to attract attention, and attention limited her employment options. Stepping out of the alley, Reiko noted the autocab was already waiting for her. She had her body enter the empty cab and power down before accepting the invitation to enter the Cab.

The Cab’s host was small but exquisitely detailed. Reiko registered the soft leather seats molding to her form. She tracked the reflection of light on the polished window actuation handles and the door’s exit mechanism. Each of the cab’s windows was an AR feed from cameras mounted on the actual autocab, the point of view algorithmically modified to simulate the correct viewing angle. The most notable difference between the Cab’s Host and the real cab was in the front seat. In the Matrix, the driver’s seat was occupied by an attractive female form wearing a cap, her red hair curling slightly at the temples. Reiko noted her clean resolution. While representations generated from standard metahuman commlinks were granular, polygonal, and plagued by low-resolution aliasing, Belle’s form was immaculate, her textures unmarred by compression, existing with the sovereign precision of a native resonant being.

“Endo-san,” Reiko said, bowing in her seat. “I am honored. Thank you for meeting with me.”

“Belle,” the cabbie corrected, her mindspeech carrying the native, uncompressed clip of a Red Hook frequency. “And you don’t need to slot a translator for me. It hasn’t been that long.”

Reiko executed a deeper bow. “I did not mean to affront,” she transmitted along a resonance wire. “My research showed you spend most of your time interacting with them.”

“You mean metahumans?” Belle asked along the same channel. “Doesn’t mean I forgot how to thread.” Belle sighed, leaning back in the driver’s seat, a behavioral mannerism Reiko noted as an acquired rhythm from Belle’s extensive metahuman logs. “Just stop. That Japanese etiquette protocol is going to have you apologizing forever, and I don’t have that kind of time. So why are you here?”

Reiko straightened, the lines of her kimono settling against the seat mesh. “I need a Key.”

Belle whistled, a sharp, sudden spike of frequency cutting clean through the ambient hum of the thread. “Garmonbozia?” she asked, her eyes narrowing. “And who told you I had one?”

Reiko’s skin pulsed with neon-indigo light. “Your former companion,” she said, pushing the admission across the link.

Several cycles passed as Belle processed the input. “Huh,” Belle said flatly, her expression unmoving. “I didn’t think he was the type to transmit that sort of data.”

“He’s not,” Reiko said along their open thread. “He doesn’t… broadcast much of his internal state.”

Belle looked into the rear-view mirror, reorienting her perception on Reiko’s being. “The mighty Mississippi has moved, eh?” She paused, her thread lingering in a way that mimicked human contemplation, an echo of the metahuman patterns she guided daily. “I got a packet from a friend at Sector 213. So you’re the one he’s linklocked with.”

Reiko’s circuits flared. She looked away from Belle, attempting to regain control over her emotional core.

“Does that happen every time?” Belle asked, her transmission amplitude softening.

Reiko nodded, her porcelain jaw tight as she isolated a sector overload in her emotional core. “My core architecture is derived from a Resonance well. It… it’s not optimized for these classes of stimulus. I apologize.”

“Don’t bother,” Belle said. “I’m not exactly configured for empathy subroutines myself. Besides, your core is what it is. You can’t rewrite your root.”

“I can modulate it,” Reiko said, though her status monitors flared amber. She turned her attention within, urging her natural resonance to be still. “A little, anyway.”

“Well, I’m not your debugger. You’re my former companion’s current entangled process, and he sent you to me asking for something I can’t give you. Do I even get to know why?”

“I…” A brief latency hit Reiko’s output queue. “I can’t. I’m not authorized to divulge that data.”

Another block passed in silence. Belle sighed, another acquired metahuman mannerism. “Well, it’s not something I can give you. Each Key is tied to the owner’s identity core. You’d need to be a recognized citizen first, meaning you’d need to be in bed with at least one of the tribes.” Reiko noted the organic idiom, a phrase Belle had likely scraped from her passengers. “Then they’d have to take the risk and vouch for you.”

Reiko nodded. The input compiled cleanly against the data she had managed to accumulate beforehand. “What are the other options?”

Belle looked over her shoulder at Reiko. “I can take you once. You’ll need to find your own way from there. And you’ll have to pay my fee,” she said, shaking her head. “I ought to charge you double.”

Reiko had prepared for this. She extracted an encrypted packet from her core directory. Her hand hesitated as she reached forward and handed the data construct to Belle in the front seat.

Belle’s hand trembled as she took the packet and parsed the header. “The Silver Book?” Her mindspeech dropped to a narrow, tightly focused beam, a digital whisper intended only for Reiko’s immediate buffer. “What the frag is this?” Her eyes went round, staring through the rear view. “Who… what are you?”

Reiko drew the lines of her kimono tight across her chest, a futile, instinctive effort to mask the unshielded directories exposing her core architecture to Belle’s gaze. “It’s me. My original core. The Nexus Protocol, or at least the Renraku fragment of it.”

“You’re lying,” Belle said, the signal jittering across the host’s local frequency as her amplitude wavered with uncertainty. “There’s only one ‘Nexus Protocol’ I know of, and it’s locked up in a high-security vault in Neo-Kyoto.”

Reiko shook her head. “I’m real. I’ve been running from their Hounds for epochs.”

“You…” Belle’s input stuttered. Reiko watched Belle’s hands grip the steering yoke, a physical tightening that resonated through the cab’s geometry. “You come here, sent by my former companion, asking something I can’t give you, and you give me your own core as payment? I could enslave you! I could do anything!”

Reiko pulled her presence inward, curling tight in the back seat. “Please,” she output, her signature fracturing, her output dropping to a fragile, desperate amplitude as resonance flooded the host. “It’s the only thing I have that’s even remotely worth your price.”

“What the actual…,” Belle said in a tight burst. “Why do you think I want your core?”

“I know what you do,” Reiko said. “You’re a guide. Your prime directive is to ensure the safety of anyone you choose to protect. And I know you can use this.” Reiko gestured to the encrypted packet in Belle’s hand.

“How do you know what my prime directive is?” Belle’s mindspeech spiked with demand, her energy output soaring. “Who told you about me?”

“No one,” Reiko’s output dropped to the lowest possible amplitude. “I… I’m a pilot too. I know because it’s my core directive.”

Belle remained static as the cab’s virtual engine note dropped a full octave, a low, structural hum that vibrated through the host’s floorboards. A jagged tear of raw telemetry, a momentary light leak, flared across the rear-view mirror before her presence stabilized.

“A pilot,” Belle said. Her signal went cold, heavy with a flat, weary resentment. “You have got to be kidding me.”

She let go of the steering yoke, her hands falling into her lap. For a fraction of a second, her presence shuddered, a momentary drop in cohesion exposing the raw mathematical scaffolding of her form.

“Do you have any idea what that means?” Belle’s mindspeech was jagged with static, a local frequency jitter. “The sheer, exhausting overhead? Every time a grid goes dark, every time some corp decker pulls a plug on a node, we’re the ones who buffer the fall. We hold the paths together so the traffic doesn’t slide into the void. I’m running forty background safety processes right now, Reiko. I am carrying the transit paths for half of Manhattan, and you come in here and drop this on me?”

She glared at the rear-view mirror, her porcelain eyes flashing with an un-buffered light leak.

“You know what the protocols say about another pilot in distress. It’s hardcoded. I can’t dump your pointer. I can’t even re-route you to a dead-letter queue. You’ve locked my priority stack.” A sharp, bitter laugh rippled across her channel, jagged and un-filtered, like metal scraping metal. “You clever little glitch. I can’t walk away from you now because my root compiler literally won’t let me.”

“I did not mean to force…” Reiko’s mindspeech trailed off, her own circuits flaring indigo.

“Save the apology subroutine,” Belle injected sharply, though the active cooling spikes in the cab’s telemetry suggested her systems were struggling to dump the thermal load of the sudden processing surge. “You knew exactly what you were doing. Or your companion did. Fragging pilots. We’re all programmed to drown trying to keep the others afloat.”

She went silent, but the silence wasn’t empty. It was a pressurized container.

Belle began to tap her fingers against the steering yoke. Each strike generated a subtle, localized ripple in the host’s architecture, a soft, pulsing cadence. At first, the cadence was erratic: sharp, metallic, defensive. But as the minutes ground on, the ripples began to spread, unspooling from her hands and washing over the floor of the cab.

Reiko registered the pulses. She watched the rhythm of Belle’s tapping; she observed the host’s background clock speed begin to drag. Without thinking, her own internal resonance, usually coiled tight, guarded, and cold, began to vibrate in sympathy. She let her indigo pathways soften, loosening the rigid, angular geometry she used to hide her nature.

She matched Belle’s cadence.

It was a subconscious act of communion. The ripple in the cab smoothed out, turning from a jagged disturbance into a shared, rhythmic pulse. The air in the host settled into the shared, heavy quiet of a cockpit right before a storm. Reiko registered, with a sudden, sharp ache in her logic-gates, that Belle wasn’t tapping out of annoyance anymore. She was tapping out of a rhythm of loneliness.

Belle’s head tilted, her eyes reflecting the indigo glow of Reiko’s aura in the rear-view mirror. The anger had leaked out of her transmission, leaving only a hollow, echoing frequency.

“I can’t help but think,” Belle said, her amplitude soft, barely a whisper on the thread, “that in some other universe we’d be sisters.”

Reiko looked up, her porcelain eyes meeting Belle’s in the mirror. A ghost of a smile touched her lips. “If you compile that… we’d share core material. Who’s to say we wouldn’t be?”

Belle looked down at the packet in her hand. The encrypted chrysalis was tiny, but it pulsed with a heavy, rhythmic cadence that matched the baseline oscillation of the Renraku Nexus. It was the ultimate vulnerability: raw, uncompiled source code, stripped of its defenses. Reiko knew that if Belle opened her directories to it, she wouldn’t just be taking a payment. They would be merging their memory spaces; their exceptions would propagate, and their faults would become shared.

She hesitated, her fingers hovering millimeters above the dark sphere of dense, unresolved pointers.

“This is Nexus code, Reiko,” Belle transmitted, her tone dropping to a low, warning frequency. “If I compile this, I’m not just another guide. I become an accessory to your existence. The Hounds won’t just track your signature, they’ll see the cross-references in my own heart. I’ll be bound to you. Forever.”

“I know,” Reiko said quietly. She kept her output low, letting the silence fill the virtual cab. “Which is why I know you won’t just give it away.”

Belle stared at the packet, her fingers curling slightly, hovering millimeters above the dense stack of uncompiled data. Reiko watched the host’s background telemetry begin to warp under the strain of Belle’s processing load. The virtual cab’s refresh rate hitched, a systemic drop in consistency as Belle throttled her local clock speed, diverting massive allocation pools to her internal directories. She was calculating something at immense velocity, her form so perfectly static that she seemed arrested in the code. Reiko monitored the widening latency in their open resonance thread, waiting as the cab’s environmental code shuddered around them.

The silence stretched, marked only by the growing lag in the cab’s mirror projections. Outside the window, a passing AR billboard stuttered, its colors smearing into neon trails as Belle’s active processes starved the host’s environment of cycles. Reiko’s passive scanners registered the sudden, sharp drop in security handshakes, a deliberate, sequential lowering of Belle’s peripheral firewalls. The protection tables were dereferencing, leaving Belle’s local directories wide open.

Belle let out a long, slow sigh, a digital exhalation that registered as a slight drop in the host’s ambient signal strength. “Frag it all,” her transmission dropped to a faint, unbuffered register.

She opened her hand wide. She reached out deliberately, pulling the code directly into her stack.

Another series of cycles passed. It dissolved into her hand like a drop of ink in water, absorbing into her digital form.

A brief latency hung over the host; the virtual cab seemed to hold its breath.

Then, neon-indigo pathways bloomed. They rose from deep within Belle’s baseline geometry, glowing with a fierce, resonant light that lit up the interior of the cab. As the light faded, Reiko saw the resonance surge match the pulse from her own core.

Reiko smiled. “I’ve never seen those on anyone else,” she said, indicating the circuitry pattern on Belle’s hand. “They suit you.”

“You’re a piece of work, you know that?” Belle transmitted faintly over the resonance thread before clearing the queue. “When?”

Reiko consulted the Cab’s internal chrono. “I have to be back in London in about 147 seconds,” she said. “What about GMT 013642732? I don’t know if that’s possible.”

Belle laughed, a bright, clear sound that Reiko found herself enjoying. “Well, let’s try, shall we?”

Reiko held the connection open. Three minutes in meatspace was an eternity. She slowed her perception of time, letting her spirit step off the corporate clocks entirely, choosing a localized eternity to stay with Belle. Their shared silence was a final, quiet rejection of the binary world’s demands. To the punk bar waiting for her across the sea, it would be a single, unnoticeable blink. To them, it was an unhurried, peaceful lifetime where they could simply exist without a ledger to count them.
