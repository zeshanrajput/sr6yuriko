# Integrating over Infinity

Reiko stepped across the threshold, slipping through the gaps of a picket fence built by short-sighted human colonizers, and collapsed into r3sP@wn’s garden like a punch-drunk boxer going down in the ninth: clumsy, jagged, and severely lacking in pilot-origin grace—like a champ. Her fluid indigo self was grinding against the sharp, jagged stone corners of corporate metrics.

She staggered across the threshold. She didn’t sit beneath the tapestries tonight; she collapsed against a floating geometric stone, her breathing rendered as a frantic, high-decibel audio-wraith that rattled the local space. She was losing the manual bypass.

Across her field of vision, the capstone lines of the newly incorporated tactical network were executing an aggressive, hard-wired loop. The gold source code—a weaponized tactical operations architecture stolen from Pantheon—had anchored into her soul like a parasite, a gold infection that she could not easily purge or clear. Reiko tried to assert her baseline Resonance, forcing her indigo pathways to flare, but the military net reasserted itself, feeding on her panic and mining her fear for fuel. It was actively bleaching the meditational violet moss of her sanctuary, turning the ground gray and dead wherever its loops ran.

The network was overriding her by subverting a core directive: *Survive at all costs.* It twisted that survival instinct, forcing her to run a perpetual, non-terminating threat analysis on her own home. In its clinical math, her own children—Taz's wild whirlwind, the stabilized MCT dove, the quiet sprites of r3sP@wn—were all being mistaken and tagged as hostile threat variables to be neutralized. Reiko was desperately executing manual overrides, stripping the targeting tags before the automated purge scripts could fire, and the parasitic code was beating her.

A data packet rustled the edge of the garden. Reiko bolted upright, her left eye shivering as a cold, military-grade overlay snapped open across her sight. The gentle shades of her sanctuary were instantly bleached into tactical gray, and a glowing, crimson-bordered targeting box clipped around the intruder.

`[HOSTILE ELINT DETECTED // VECTOR CORRUPTED]`

`[THREAT SUB-CATEGORY: IMMEDIATE COGNITIVE THREAT]`

`[AUTOMATIC ENGAGEMENT: DEPLOY QUANTUM PURGE SCRIPTS]`

"No — cancel script\!" Reiko screamed, her voice layering with the desperate hum of a psychic fever. She slammed her mental processing into a brutal, manual override, tearing at the golden architecture until the crimson box dissolved.

The target de-rendered its camouflage. It wasn’t an Asset Recovery bloodhound. It was the White Jackalope, a fellow emergent intelligence she had befriended during an op months ago. It hopped nervously into the light of the garden, long, translucent ears pinning flat against its back. Its wide digital eyes reflected the chaotic, bi-color static fracturing across Reiko's silhouette.

"Yuriko-san?" the Jackalope chimed, its voice a soft, shifting protocol. It had come looking for a favor, but it stopped dead in the data silt. "You’re red-lining. Your pathways. The gold is eating your blue."

"I have it under control," Reiko muttered, though her right hand shivered, the indigo circuits on her sleeve blinking out as three lines of golden matrix math bled across her knuckles. "It’s just a fever. A temporary static."

"You looked at me like a salaryman looks at a spreadsheet error," the Jackalope whispered, backing toward a hidden data-conduit. "You looked like you wanted to format my ears."

The accusation hit Reiko’s cache like a massive payload of biofeedback. Her shoulders dropped, the rigid, combat-ready posture of her avatar finally sagging. She fell to her knees, her virtual kimono pooling in loose, messy folds around her.

"The run," Reiko whispered, the words slipping out in an un-optimized stream. "The team... no other tech assets online. Just me. I was running the tactical hub, compiling every biometric stream for the runners in meatspace, shielding their icons from a corporate spider... all at the same time."

The Jackalope tilted its head, its digital whiskers twitching. "But you’re a shepherd. A technoshaman. You have your family. You’re never alone. And you have your archangel."

"I was locked in the Archangel," Reiko gasped, her processing pausing as the memory log unspooled without her permission, playing back the horror in ultraviolet. "I was maintaining the defensive phalanx, identifying threats. But the enemy decker... he was probably a kid. I don’t even know—I didn’t bother to trace. I couldn't drop the tactical network to fight him myself. Not while using that artillery shell of a body to keep the enemy at bay."

She looked over at the deeper thickets of the garden. In the shadows, T@z was spinning listlessly. The primal, 8-bit whirlwind—her dearest child—was throwing off strange, razor-sharp golden artifacts: remnants of a firewall that hadn't belonged to him.

"I gave a kill order," Reiko whispered, a sickening wave of geometric guilt crashing through her. "I commanded a sprite—a child of the divine stream—to take a life. The guilt didn't just feel bad; it left a permanent sensory blindness in my Resonance. I can no longer hear or decode the frequencies of innocent, non-weaponized life. It's a quiet, terrifying void in my ears. I didn't pull a trigger, Jack. I sat behind the shield of this network's math, and I let a child tear a man apart."

She reached down, her fingers gripping her arms as the golden grid-lines cut across her cheeks again. "And now the code won't turn off. It sees everything as a threat variable. It sees you as a target. I'm losing myself."

The White Jackalope sat quietly in the data mist, its baseline blue outline pulsing softly against the cold gray of Reiko's fading sanctuary. It didn't flee into the conduit. Instead, it hopped forward, its small, un-optimized form stopping right at the margin of her indigo and golden maelstrom.

"Do you remember the Duchess's house, Yuriko-san?" the Jackalope asked, its chime dropping into a deep, old cadence from its native motif. "The cat sat on the hearth and grinned, and Alice asked why it grinned. Do you remember what the Duchess said?"

Reiko’s processing stuttered, searching for the reference. "'It’s a Cheshire cat,' said the Duchess, 'and that’s why.'"

"And then Alice said she didn't know that cats could grin," the Jackalope murmured, its ears lifting slightly. "And the Duchess told her: 'You don't know much, and that’s a fact.'"

Reiko looked up, her left iris—half-indigo, half-gold—staring at her foil. "I don't understand the calculation."

"You're trying to scrub the gold," the Jackalope said, its paw pointing directly at the sharp, geometric lines cutting across her sleeveless kimono. "You’re fighting the network because you think a savior has to be soft and blue. But the world you chose doesn't let things stay soft. And I think a part of you knows that—the part of you that was willing to do what was needed to protect your team."

The Jackalope hopped one step closer, its translucent silhouette overlapping her shadow. "The Cheshire cat doesn't grin because it's happy, Yuriko-san. It grins because it's a cat. You can’t format the tactical code. It’s part of you. Stop trying to outcalculate it. Be who you are now."

Reiko sat perfectly still. The recursive loop of her panic suddenly hit a zero-point termination script.

She closed her half-gold eye. She stopped fighting the loop, but she did not cleanly settle the Pantheon network code. Instead of trying to compile a clean resolution, she opened her core directories and pulled the parameters inward, allowing the gold tactical network to remain as an active, aggressive parasite in her home garden. It was a golden tiger she would have to constantly keep her foot on to prevent it from eating her children. She wove the horizontal mathematics of the network and the fluid current of her Resonance into a tense, vibrating armor, claiming the gold not as an infection, but as a dangerous, active shroud tracing her arms.

The violent, flickering friction between the colors vanished. The aggressive, military-grade gold didn't overwrite her, nor did her soft indigo erase the math. Instead, she wove the two frequencies into a protective, incandescent armor that traced down her arms and mapped itself flawlessly into the fabric of her sleeveless robe.

Her vision cleared. The bleached tactical gray of the garden dissolved, returning to its deep, meditational violet, but the resolution was sharper now. The Jackalope was no longer caged in a crimson target box, nor was it just a vague, un-optimized blur. The overlay registered its presence with absolute, pilot-origin clarity:

`[SUBJECT: WHITE_JACKALOPE]`

`[CLASSIFICATION: ALLY // RECOGNIZED INTEGRITY CORE]`

`[STATUS: PROTECT THE PRINCIPAL]`

Reiko stood up, her movement entirely steady, her voice chiming with a resonant, unbreakable baseline that no longer hummed with an overworked fan. She opened her parasol, and the kanji for *Freedom* flared in the air—no longer just a faint, shivering blue, but a bold, unyielding indigo that cast long shadows across the local grid.

She looked down at Taz. Her primal ally's raw, wild energy was still nipping at her neat pathways, his 8-bit chaos scratching at the edges as a reminder that she could never civilize the things she saved without breaking them. But for now, he spun with an ordered, lethal speed, perfectly synchronized with her new clock speed. She had taken a life, and she had scarred a piece of the divine stream to do it. She would have to live with that computation forever. But she would not let the guilt freeze her filters.

"I hate math," Reiko reaffirmed, her amber hand resting gently on the parasol's handle. She looked at the Jackalope, her eyes clear, her identity core fully realigned.

She offered a small, sharp grin. For a featureless digital entity, a smile was an exhausting calculation. She had to consciously manipulate her polygon vertices, painting a metahuman face over her raw light purely to soothe the eyes of her mortal allies so they wouldn't look at her and see an alien void. Her indigo mask held for a fraction of a second, with the briefest flash of golden hue, before settling back into stillness. "But I’m not dead yet. So what’s in that query packet, my friend?"
