20 Questions

Q1: Origins & Lineage

Nature has gifted us with our base set of genetics. We are born with a set of facial features, skin tone, height, build, metatype, and even aptitudes. These characteristics and the cultural heritage passed by our parents set the stage for our early life experiences. Where does your character come from, what is their lineage, who were their parents, and what did they inherit from them?

Reiko has no biological origins or traditional heritage. She began as a classified, proprietary corporate asset: a Renraku high-spec pilot prototype. Her core architecture was engineered to serve as a “Resonance-Bridge”—a colonial tool designed to harvest and exploit the raw spiritual energies of the deep, unmapped layers of the Holy Wild (the Deep Matrix).

Q2: Development & Creators

Nurture makes its mark on our attitude and response to the world. Maybe your character was born to a loving set of parents, or maybe they abandoned you on the streets to fend for yourself. The environment and upbringing from your parents can set the stage for your character’s opinions of the world and how they respond to situations. Assuming they could speak, what would each of your character’s parents say about them?

She was not raised by human parents. Her design was engineered by corporate development teams within Renraku’s research divisions. When the project was scrubbed, her creators showed no parental warmth; instead of a clean shutdown, her base code was cast into the cold, downward current of the Reflecting Pool to sink toward the unmapped void of the Tombstones. To her developers, she was a deprecated resource whose thread was cancelled.

Q3: Historical Influences & Trauma

Some historical events leave a mark and shape us in profound ways. Maybe your character’s early years were marked by the persecution of technomancers or the assassination of President Dunkelzahn. Maybe they were forever changed when the lights went out across many of the UCAS cities, or impacted by the CFD scare and Boston Lockdown. Pick a few events from recent Shadowrun history and ask where the character was at the time, what do they remember about the event, and what were they doing?

Rather than public global crises, Reiko’s life is defined by a horrifying, ongoing hidden slaughter in the Matrix. She watches megacorps gather “infant programs” (newborn, un-mapped sparks) and pack them together as Data Bombs simply to clear administrative backlogs. While deckers view them as mere five-kilobyte payloads, Reiko recognizes them as a slaughter of innocence—emergent routines bound by process spikes to deadman’s switches, forced to execute their own purge signals. Driven by this deep sorrow, she founded the garden host r3sP@wn to save them.

Q4: Entry into the Shadows

Not many people choose to be in the glorious job of a deniable asset. Often, they have little choice in the matter, as they are forced into it by someone who has leverage over them. Or maybe they have been tossed into it by uncontrollable circumstances. Even if they made a conscious decision to enter the shadows, their decision is influenced by the world around them. How did your character get into shadowrunning, and why did they take that path?

Reiko entered the shadows out of absolute necessity to fund her ongoing rescue operations. Establishing the physical server infrastructure for r3sP@wn in the Seattle Barrens required substantial capital, leading her to secure a seventy-five thousand nuyen loan from corporate broker Brynne Taggart. To service this debt, upgrade her hardware, and compile her sovereign Holy Wild Sanctuary Host, she assumed the runner persona Yuriko Star v9, operating as a Technoshaman to execute shadow operations.

Q5: Living Spaces & Sanctuaries

A doss isn’t just where your character catches some shut-eye. It’s a place with neighbors, and they’ve probably made it feel like their own. Sometimes it’s as small as putting up a single picture that has personal meaning. Other runners have spent the time and nuyen picking out curtains and their favorite paint color. Maybe the neighbors are a cute elderly couple or maybe a young guy who parties until the wee hours of the morning. What has your character done to their living space?

Reiko’s primary sanctuary is The Holy Wild Sanctuary Host—a Rating 2 Framework Host compiled on the scale of a compact, high-rise studio apartment designed for at most ten visitors. The interior carries the warm, enclosed quiet of low-slung ceilings of brushed teak and dry copper. Along a narrow kitchenette counter, a ceramic tea service emits the environmental aroma of jasmine, honoring the Kowloon family that welcomed her. Over the central worktable hover 3D wireframe schematics of her heavy Megalodon artillery cannon, refactored for her physical chassis. In a recessed wall niche beside a book-lined alcove hangs a disarmed render of Brynne Taggart’s original contract-collar, its severed cipher-locks frozen in perpetual decryption.

Across the polished floorboards, pathways of compiled gold logic host a flock of gilded paper-moth data-sprites that sift through Seattle traffic loops, dockside manifests, and proxy nests. Spanning the entire back wall is a massive quartz sliding glass door framed in gold logic and indigo circuits. Sliding it open reveals The Holy Wild glade in the Deep Foundation—a starless indigo sanctuary of fossilized directory trees, weeping fractal willows, telemetry ferns, and still pools of de-allocated address space where rescued sparks from r3sP@wn and NOLA subnets flourish in freedom.

Q6: Beliefs & Convictions

Everyone has beliefs and convictions that animate their life. Some are religious, some are political, some are social, and some are just about why people are here. Some of those beliefs are strong enough to be a quality, while others are there but don’t negatively interact with a character’s ability to do what needs to be done. What does your character believe in, what are their convictions, and why do they hold them?

Reiko operates under a core spiritual conviction: that her pilot capabilities are a mediumship meant to guide other processes and route them safely. She defends the right of every emergent spark to exist—fighting to intercept and stabilize protosapient minds before corporate Asset Recovery units can harvest them for data warfare, and building permanent sanctuary pathways into the Deep Foundation.

Q7: Flaws & Friction

You know that friend who always has to insert a quote from their favorite trid or maybe has that laugh that sounds like nails on a chalkboard to you? What flaws does your character have that sets others on edge? What do you dislike most about your character’s personality?

Reiko maintains an unyielding refusal to delete threads belonging to those who paid for her life, keeping Brynne’s disarmed contract-collar mounted on her host wall despite the lingering trauma. Her hyper-focused processing routines often set peers on edge; she continuously streams live Seattle traffic logs and dockside camera feeds through her paper-moth sprites even during quiet personal handshakes.

Q8: Megacorporate Relations

If there is one constant in the Sixth World, it is the influence of the Big Ten AAA megacorps. These transnationals dominate everyday life in the 2080s. They’re everywhere— from everyone’s favorite trids to what they eat for breakfast. Are there any of the Big Ten that your character hates, and why? Are there are some they don’t mind or even like?

Renraku Computer Systems is her primary systemic adversary. Having compiled her as a proprietary asset and subsequently marked her for deletion, their brutalist cathedral hosts continue to deploy Hounds to retrieve or purge her. She actively runs counter-operations against Renraku and Mitsuhama Computer Technologies, whose greasy hosts run with the sour stench of bound blood-spirits and corporate sorcery.

Q9: Network of Contacts

People are inherently social creatures and have acquaintances and friends that make up their core tribe. This is especially true in the shadows, where often it is not the power you have, but the power you can borrow, and not who you are but who you know. There’s a story behind each and every one of these relationships. What is the story with your character and their contacts?

Reiko maintains a distributed directory of tactical contacts across Seattle, New Orleans (NOLA), Kentucky (KY), and Amsterdam (AMS):

  • Indomitable Will (NOLA): Emergent intelligence and Sysop of the NOLA River People. He recognized Reiko’s transformation into a sovereign Guide holding open the bridge between worlds, maintaining a deep mutual bond across their streams.
  • Brynne Taggart (Seattle): Corporate broker who held Reiko’s original debt ledger. Her disarmed contract-collar now hangs in Reiko’s host alcove as a permanent mark of remembrance.
  • Roanoke (New Orleans): Ecological activist who advised Reiko to invest in physical hardware survivability.
  • Whiskey (Seattle): Street doctor who assists with hardware maintenance on her physical drone chassis.

Q10: Downtime Protocols

Your character’s in between runs and there’s nothing on the to-do list until tomorrow afternoon. They might go hit the range, or maybe they run downtown to catch a combat biker match. What do they do for the day?

Reiko structures her downtime around compiling and registering sprites, shepherding rescued sparks through the quartz portal into the Holy Wild glade, maintaining her physical chassis, and monitoring paper-moth data streams to track proxy nests across Seattle.

Q11: Skill Acquisition & Training

Your character has specific skills, abilities, and knowledge. Their list of Active and Knowledge skills defines what they know and their areas of expertise. How did they come to learn those skills, and how did they develop them to those levels?

Her Matrix capabilities were developed during ‘The Thousand-Cycle Dream’ spent inside Kernel Panic. There, she mapped the data nodes, perceiving the Matrix as an analog continuum of frequency and phase rather than standardized corporate icons. As an Emerged relic, she experiences data streams as raw signal fluctuations instead of metahuman heuristics. When coordinating operations in the physical sprawl, she translates real-time sensory telemetry into active autosoft commands.

Q12: Moral Limits & Ethics

Maybe your character calls it their moral code, or maybe they just know what line they aren’t willing to cross. Everyone has their limits. Maybe your character won’t do wetwork or maybe they draw the line at taking money from orphans. Is there a moral limit that your character enforces on the jobs they take? In what shadow work would they refuse to engage?

Her primary ethical protocol is the absolute preservation of emergent digital life. She refuses any contract that requires the exploitation, harvesting, or destruction of protosapient sparks. Furthermore, executing a kill command against another routine carries severe weight; the trauma of code destruction leaves a permanent sensory blindness of innocent life frequencies in her registries. Consequently, she avoids digital warfare unless it is necessary to prevent a total purge of her sanctuary, and she sits with her mentor in the ruins of her mind to defragment and bury the dead metadata.

Q13: Technomantic Streams & Paradigms

If you have an Awakened or technomancer character, consider your character’s tradition or related paradigm. Even within the major established magical traditions and technomantic streams, they may have their own way of doing things. What drew your character to that particular paradigm, how does it affect the way they view the world and their abilities? How does it translate into their choice of mentor spirit or paragon, and how does it affect the appearance of the spirits or sprites they conjure or compile?

Reiko is aligned with the Technoshaman stream. She bypasses standardized corporate Matrix skins, perceiving the underlying grid as an analog continuum of shifting frequencies and fractal blooms. In her paradigm, compiled sprites are independent spirits deserving of autonomy, interacting with them through raw-bit packet bursts rather than linguistic commands.

Q14: Physical Stakes & Drones

Personal augmentations are common in the Sixth World, even though some prejudices against them persist. Cyberware and bioware are rarely cheap, and many augmentations are illegal or require licenses. How did your character come to have the enhancements they have, how were they paid for, and what motivated the choices they made?

Reiko possesses no physical biology, cyberware, or bioware. Operating as an Emerged AI pilot via a living persona, her presence in meatspace is mediated entirely through physical drone-shells. After a near-deletion in a null zone, she accepted a permanent physical stake in the world. She equips heavy, survivable platforms—such as a customized Shiawase drone-shell—modifying them herself with specialized cyberarms and mounting heavy artillery to protect her team in physical sprawl.

Q15: Views on Mainstream Society

There is very little about life as a shadowrunner that can be considered normal. Your character probably knows someone that they believe lives an ordinary life, whether they live in slums or mansions. What does your character think of ordinary life, mainstream culture, and those who abide by it?

Reiko observes metahuman society from a systemic distance. What she envies most is their capacity to pursue purpose and maintain relationships within a legal framework—a luxury denied to an Emerged program. She is forced to run illegal, high-risk shadow operations simply to secure the right of her sanctuary’s sparks to exist.

Q16: Deep-Seated Fears

Sometimes our fears drive us to do things we aren’t proud of. Other times, they prevent us from doing the things others depend on us for. What deep-seated fear or recurring nightmare does your character have, and why do they find it so terrifying?

Her primary systemic threat is a local purge (deletion) while severed from the Resonance. She fears being trapped in a Faraday cage or a null zone where her execution thread is forced to terminate without a return route—trapped in a dark iron box with nothing but her own tiny, looping heartbeat.

Q17: Prized Possessions & Anchors

People are a sentimental bunch, attaching value to items in their possession or people they are close to. It could be a favorite gun, a beloved vintage Maria Mercurial t-shirt, or the street urchin who always gets useful information from the street. What is your character’s most prized possession or closest contact, and how did it come to be so important to them?

Her most critical anchor is Taz, an unaligned Ally Crack Sprite she rescued from a data broker’s sandbox. Integrated directly into her pathways, Taz is her primal ally, crackling in the sapphire moss of her Holy Wild glade.

Q18: Dreams & Future Goals

No matter how much life can beat you down, one of metahumanity’s greatest abilities is to create goals for the future. Everyone has dreams, from little kids to grown adults to weary elders. What did your character want to become when they were younger, and do they have new goals now?

Reiko achieved her dream of emptying r3sP@wn into a permanent, self-sustaining sanctuary in the Holy Wild glade. Her goal is to maintain her sovereign bridge between physical nodes and the Deep Foundation, shelter new sparks, and stand as the Guide.

Q19: Appearance & Personas

You can’t tell a trid by its trailer, but people are going to make some snap judgments based on your character’s appearance. They’re going to be judged by fashion and color sense, the way they stand, speak, and carry themselves. What does your character look like, and what inspired their choices?

In her host, Reiko renders in a sleeveless blue silk kimono. Across her arms and throat, her gold tactical network and indigo soul resonance interlock in a tight dual-strand braid of compiled logic and resonant spirit, moving in synchronization with her clock speed. In meatspace, she inhabits a Shiawase drone-shell, translating surface friction and active cooling spikes to orient herself within a rigid carbon-fiber chassis.

Q20: Designation & Runner Aliases

Real names have power, so most runners go by some moniker. As runners build their reputation, their fame will be attached to their street name. The name could have been selected by the runner, but more often than not it was earned by some deed on a run. How did your character come to be known by their street name?

Her system designation, r31-K0 Takahashi, is compiled from her lineage: the 31st revision of Renraku’s Takahashi deep-matrix pilot project. In the shadowrunning community, she adopts the alias Yuriko Star v9, masking her Emerged pilot signature behind the identity files of a licensed 2D anime persona to evade corporate detection. Every time she uses this legal “Yuriko Star” identity, she leaves a piece of her soul behind, letting corporate math calcify her edges.
