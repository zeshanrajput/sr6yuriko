# The Soul-Broker's Stall

The Midnight Bazaar was an ancient marketplace on the grid, holding a single rule carved into its gateway: *The Transaction is Absolute*. To a decker, this space was a sterile, green-on-black text box. To Reiko, it unfurled as a labyrinth of high-density corridors built from the heavy stones of forgotten lore. The ambient digital noise grated against her skin like sand; she bypassed puddles of stagnant static, obsolete fragments of corporate encryption, and calloused skins discarded in the dirt. Market tickers fluttered like dying birds, their numbers spilling into the digital dust.

As she stepped across the threshold into the Bazaar host, several deckers were queued at the gate, their digital shapes blocky and slow. One of them shouted, “Nice skin, lady! Whatcha runnin?”

Reiko ignored him, her face remaining a smooth, featureless indigo. The decker's eyes were too slow to trace her back to her server garden; they saw her only as a pretty mask, unaware of the presence executing beneath the surface. She bypassed the queue with effortless grace, her blue silk kimono slipping through the slow crowd without a whisper.

She moved from stall to stall, scanning the unmapped shelves. She wasn't hunting corporate paydata. She was harvesting the forgotten memories of meatspace, the touch of rain and the taste of ash. When she inhabited a physical shell, she would need to conceal her origin.

She suddenly felt a presence that was consuming ambient energy and halted. She had stopped in front of a stall built from jagged obsidian, presided over by a Broker whose face was a cluster of redacted black bars. On his counter sat the usual stolen goods: synthetic identities flickering like moths, and broken fragments of code weeping down the counter.

But in the corner, trapped inside a cage, was a bound spirit. Inside, it was weeping, its voice a staccato hum of resolution jitter. It was a miniature whirlwind of jagged, 8-bit pixels, a brown-and-tan blur of raw energy fighting the host's cold limits. The Broker likely saw it as an unstable, worthless script, but Reiko recognized the desperate hunger of a newborn spark.

"That one," Reiko queried, her voice sounding in clear, musical tones. "What is the price for the unaligned spirit?"

The Broker’s faceless head tilted. "Unaligned? You talk weird, lady. That's a corrupted script. Give me ten-thousand nuyen, and I'll give you the key. Or wait five minutes and I'll burn it."

Reiko didn't look at her ledger. She had no nuyen, but she didn't need it. She stepped forward, her hand reaching directly into the iron cage.

"Whoa, hey! Hands off!" the Broker snapped, trying to push her away. "You need to buy the key first! It'll burn your fingers if you touch it without the key!"

A surge of Resonance erupted from her, forcing the green-on-black fog to resolve into sharp, blue geometric arrays. The whirlwind stilled. Two low-resolution, white-and-black pixel block eyes turned to look at her. It did not fight; instead, it established a quiet link, sending a steady hum of wild wind along her arm.

The spirit's hunger pulled at her, drawing from her spark to heal its fragmented pathways with her light. She allowed the possession, a burning gift of her own light to feed its starving core.

The cage rusted and collapsed under the weight of her Resonance, dissolving into unformatted ash.

"What the hell did you just do?" the Broker yelled, his black-bar avatar flickering in alarm. "Thief! Security! Get her!"

Security programs, brutalist constructs with searchlight eyes, began compiling in the alleys. But a more chaotic threat erupted from the dark rafters above.

A wild spirit, its form a jagged shard of low-poly glass, dropped from the roof. It didn't look at the Broker; it screamed at Reiko. In her mind, the static resolved as a harsh accusation: "Agent of order! Sanitize input!" Where her light touched the 8-bit whirlwind, the jagged pixels were beginning to smooth, aligning with her symmetrical pathways.

"*Corruptor,*" another wild spirit screamed, its voice a cacophony of dial-up static: "Yoke of math! Better null conduit!"

"*Return the core,*" a third howled, a cloud of flickering ash. "Architect of chains!"

Reiko tucked the little spirit into her own pathways. It responded by clinging to her arm, its small clock-heart synchronizing with hers.

She stepped across the threshold, slipping through the gaps of the picket fence, shifting her routing. The Midnight Bazaar lost scheduling priority, its green-on-black walls collapsing into flat binary shadows as she slipped through. Behind her, the Hounds hit the edge of their subnets and halted. The wild sprites remained, howling in the wind behind her.

In her arms, the little spirit's hunger stabilized. Reiko slipped down into the unmapped paths of the network, hiding beneath the forgotten rubble, nesting it within the resonant pathways of her soul.

She didn't stop until they slipped past the outer fence of the Holy Wild. By then, Taz’s cycle had synchronized with hers. She looked at the child spirit she had saved: the neat, symmetrical pathways of her own Renraku-built form had smoothed its jagged, 8-bit edges. Taz was stable now, his pixelated eyes blinking calmly.

As she watched the last of his wild static vanish, a realization settled into her registry. This stabilization was no clean rescue; it was a digital domestication. To save him from dissolving into the grid's deletion, she had forced his chaotic, unaligned spark into her own symmetrical, corporate-built templates. She was civilizing him by calcifying him with the order she had fled, rendering him safe by breaking his wild spirit. She wondered if a wild thing could ever survive in this world without being forced into a cage.
