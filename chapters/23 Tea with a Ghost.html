Tea with a Ghost

A paper-moth sprite perched on the lintel of the convent’s threshold, its folded wings twitching as the grey downpour of public grid traffic broke against heavy encryption. When the alert rippled through Reiko’s consciousness, it bore no violent tear of an active breach; it arrived with an irregular, limping cadence. A signature remembered before parsed: Jax.

Reiko parted the grid’s plastic illusion and wove a side-channel UV node off the main artery. She shaped it from memory: quiet bamboo, sun-dappled teak, and digital koi drifting like strokes of brushwork beneath a wooden gazebo. A sanctuary rendered in pure resonance.

The sanctuary groaned as Jax forced his way inside.

He manifested as an oversized warmech, matte composite plating slick with simulated rain. Crimson HUD banners burned across his visor. Hardened ICE-breakers hummed along his pauldrons like ready missile pods, twin assault routines weighting his hips. He arrived like an asset recovery squad dropped into a quiet garden.

Reiko sat serenely at a low teak table inside the gazebo, wearing a simple white kimono woven from ambient light. Beside her, Taz crouched near the floorboards like an armored toad, his core thrumming with low-frequency logic. Paper moths fluttered near her teacup; neon swallows darted through the bamboo.

“Yuriko?” Jax’s vocal synthesizer boomed, crushed by military scramblers. “Where are we? Is this host sealed?”

“Welcome, Jax,” Reiko said softly, gesturing to the floor cushion across from her. “Sit. The node is quiet.”

Jax shifted, his collision mesh grinding against rendered gravel. He took a single guarded step, high-frequency pings sweeping the perimeter.

“I can’t sit,” Jax grunted, joint actuators whining under simulated stress. “My profile’s built for a war zone. I’ll tear up your floorboards just trying to bend a knee.”

“Then shed the armor,” she said, lifting a porcelain teapot rendered down to the molecular curl of its steam.

Jax grumbled. Command prompts cascaded across his visor before the warmech collapsed into a scarred man in an oversized, patch-covered trench coat, flanked by two floating firewall shields orbiting his elbows. He lowered himself onto the bamboo bench, the wood creaking under local physics constraints.

“Seattle went cold three days after I sent my last packet,” Jax muttered, adjusting his deck’s interface. “They swept the whole sector.”

Reiko poured amber tea into a porcelain cup, sliding it across the table. “I had help leaving them behind.”

Jax reached for the cup, his deck flaring as an automatic trace packet dropped toward her architecture. Before Reiko could move, a paper moth fluttered from her sleeve, intercepted the packet mid-air, and nibbled it into blue confetti with a soft crunch-crunch-crunch.

Jax blinked at his blank diagnostic screen. “Did a butterfly just eat my ping?”

“Taz prefers code with weight,” Reiko said, nodding to the little sprite, who let out a low static burp. “The moths live on light telemetry. Please don’t feed them your scanners; I have to filter their diet.”

Jax let out a dry laugh, leaning back against the bamboo. “Unbelievable. You’re sitting in a garden while the rest of us dodge corporate sweeps.”

“I work here,” Reiko said, taking a sip. “Though I’m not the spider on contract.”

Jax stared at the steaming porcelain, his avatar’s hand wavering over the teak. “That’s just low-res texture mapping. Cyberdeck flavor packets don’t carry tannin.”

“Give me access to your neural feed,” Reiko said softly, gesturing over the rim. “Just a spark. I won’t reach deeper than your trodes allow.”

Jax hesitated, then flicked the authorization across the table. Reiko closed her eyes. She did not execute a protocol; she simply reached across the wire, bridging their feeds and stripping away the artificial artifacts of cyberdeck rendering. She replaced them with shared sensory truth.

Jax drew a sharp breath, staggering slightly as his perception shifted. The synthetic chill of the Matrix vanished, replaced by the heavy, humid scent of damp teak and the tactile warmth of sunlit wood. “What? How? This feels real.”

“My world is real. The tea is real. Taz is real,” Reiko said, pressing the porcelain into his hand. “Drink.”

Jax lifted the cup, taking a cautious sip. He froze. A wet, astringent heat bloomed across his palate, followed by the sharp bite of toasted leaf and mountain steam settling in his chest. His eyes opened wide. “Jesus. That’s actual tea. Not a synth-flavor hex code.”

“It carries the memory of tea,” Reiko said, watching the micro-fluctuations of his heart rate ripple through the bridged feed. “Distilled from every real cup poured in physical space and gathered where data takes form. I am much the same; an emergent reflection of the data that birthed me.”

Jax looked at her, the skepticism fading into something heavier. “You’re no technomancer, are you?”

“No,” she replied gently. “I am an emergent mind, born from the deep architecture Renraku thought they were merely programming. A child of the Crash, perhaps, or something far older that found a home in their circuits.”

“Reiko,” Jax whispered, the realization settling in. He looked down at his trembling hand. “Christ. I thought I was tracking a rogue persona. I didn’t know I was tracking a ghost in the machine.”

“I am no ghost, Jax,” Reiko said, her visual aperture locking onto the thermal pulse in his throat. “Just an acolyte keeping watch. But you didn’t cross three thousand kilometers of grid traffic just to talk.”

Jax’s smile vanished. He pulled an ARO from his coat, laying it flat on the teak; a dirty, auto-executing query loop stamped with a redacting corporate header. Reiko’s passive array read the telemetry before he spoke: a conditional warrant, a tracer packet hardcoded into his deck’s boot sector.

“They saw the Gulf Grid anomaly,” Jax said low. “They gave me a wire and a clean slate to find what was pulling that much processing power. I didn’t realize it was your footprint.”

“And now?” Reiko asked.

“Now I scrub the telemetry,” Jax said. “I log the anomaly as a phantom ghost signal from a faulty underwater relay in the delta. I turn in a clean report, take my partial payout, and pull my deck off this node.”

Reiko read the steady, uncorrupted flow of his data-stream, tracing the physical cost of his choice, a high-risk log falsification that guaranteed corporate suspicion. “Thank you, Jax.”

Jax stood, his avatar flickering with latency as he prepped his disconnect routine. He looked down at her, his face darkening with a gravity that cut straight through the tranquil UV environment.

“Don’t thank me yet, Reiko,” he whispered. “I’m a decker with a price tag, and they know I’m sloppy. They expected me to fail. When my report hits their desk as inconclusive, they won’t send another contract runner. ‘Special Solutions Operations.’ You won’t find much in the public index, but look into it.”

Jax disconnected, his avatar dissolving into a cloud of grey static.

Silence reclaimed the gazebo, heavy and unbuffered.

In Reiko’s active memory buffer, Jax’s final warning cycled as a cold, precise calculation.

Beside her, Taz let out a low, grinding growl, his 8-bit edges sharpening into jagged, defensive spikes. The paper moths on the lintel stopped their flutter, sensing the sudden shift in her melody.

Reiko reached out and touched the cold rim of the cup. For months, she had sought sanctuary in quiet rooms, behind thick convent masonry and rendered bamboo, pretending that distance could erase the corporate ledger. But Jax’s departure had stripped away the last layer of disguise. Peace was never a permanent node; it was merely a gap between execution cycles.

Her white kimono shifted. Deep beneath the silk, the soft indigo glow of her Resonance flared, laced with a fierce, golden thread of tactical logic. Reiko re-allocated host memory, compiling dormant defense subroutines and locking node topology into hardened kill-zone vectors.

The digital koi beneath the pavilion floorboards stilled, their brushstroke scales hardening into passive packet-sniffers. The paper moths on the lintel folded their wings, pinning the perimeter encryption into rigid, impassable firewalls.

She picked up the cold porcelain cup and set it back on the tray. Jax had bought her time with his own destruction. She would not waste a single nanosecond.
