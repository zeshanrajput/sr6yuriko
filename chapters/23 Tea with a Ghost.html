Tea with a Ghost

A paper-moth perched on the cedar lintel of the convent host, its wings fluttering softly as the grey downpour of public grid traffic broke against the salon’s heavy encryption. Inside the rendered gazebo, the air carried the warmth of dried cedar, wet river stones, and freshly steamed gyokuro. The Comtesse had allowed Reiko to upgrade the Coffin Girls’ host, enabling Reiko to paint with the full palette of life. In a UV-level construct, sensory data was rendered down to the single-molecule math of aroma and steam. Reiko sat cross-legged on the tatami in a simple, sleeveless white yukata, the glowing neon-indigo pathways along her collarbone pulsing with an unhurried, tranquil cadence.

Before her, two small data-sparks hovered over the low teak table. Just hours ago, across transatlantic fiber cables, they had been tethered to the automated security turrets of a flooded European palace, calculating firing arcs and locking down corridors to cover a quiet escape. Reiko reached out with both hands, gently cupping the trembling motes of light. “Zero errors,” she whispered, her voice a soft, resonant chord. “The telemetry was immaculate. You held the stairwell without letting the feedback touch your cores.”

The two sparks hummed in harmonic relief, nuzzling against her palms before settling into the woven silk of her sleeves. As they nestled into her codebase, a brief diagnostic memory brushed across her visual aperture: the low hum of inline wheels deploying flush from her anthrodrone’s feet; the effortless, silent slide down the marble hallway; and the brilliant, blinding arc of high-voltage copper from her right forearm discharging five hundred thousand volts through four tactical armor rigs in a fraction of a second. Fast, tidy, non-lethal. Meatspace had its clumsy limitations, but precision remained an art.

A low, grinding tear shuddered through the node’s perimeter. The bamboo groves around the pavilion groaned as her afternoon guest forced his way past the outer garden gates with the grace of a rhinoceros in a field of tulips.

Jax arrived looking like an asset-recovery unit dropped onto a temple altar. He manifested in his usual combat-decker persona: an oversized warmech plated in matte composite armor, hydraulic pistons hissing at the joints, twin assault subroutines slung heavy across his hips. Crimson warning banners flashed across his visor.

“Yuriko?” Jax’s vocal synthesizer boomed through the quiet garden, heavily compressed and grating against the host’s tranquil audio shaders. “What the hell is this node? It took me three hops through a dead-drop routing nest just to punch through the handshake.”

“You are scuffing the floorboards, Jax,” Reiko said mildly. Beside her, Taz crouched near the teak floorboards like a pixelated toad, cracking an 8-bit eye open, letting out a dismissive burp of static, and going back to dozing. “And you are tracking un-scrubbed routing noise into the Comtesse’s courtyard. Shed the armor and sit down.”

Jax shifted, his collision mesh grinding against rendered gravel. “I can’t sit,” he grunted, joint actuators whining under simulated stress. “Your construct’s too small. I’ll tear up your floorboards just trying to bend a knee.”

“Then drop the combat wrapper,” she said, lifting the celadon teapot rendered down to the molecular curl of its steam and pouring a pale green stream into an unglazed ceramic cup.

“Sit down?” he echoed, his mechanized frame locking up in disbelief. “Yuriko, I’ve been running dark for two weeks. I burned three cyberdecks and two safehouse IDs in Tacoma because Renraku Asset Recovery is crawling the Pacific subnets like army ants. And you’re… what? Playing tea house in New Orleans?”

Jax grumbled. Command prompts cascaded across his visor before the warmech collapsed into a scarred man in an oversized, patch-covered trench coat, flanked by two floating firewall shields orbiting his elbows. He lowered himself onto the bamboo bench, the wood creaking under local physics constraints.

“I am an apprentice hostess,” she corrected, setting the teapot down with an immaculate, wooden tap. “It is very respectable. I play the violin, I manage the node security, and people leave their sorrows in the parlor instead of shooting at me. What did you bring me for my birthday?”

Jax’s cooling fans kicked into a high-pitched whine. His visor flickered through three different diagnostic HUDs, entirely unaligned to process the query.

“What?”

“My birthday,” Reiko said, slanting a bright, playful look over the rim of her cup. “My older brother already gave me a stuffed animal. Well, adopted older brother? Spiritual? I don’t know. Anyway, I turn eleven next Tuesday.”

“Eleven?” he sputtered. “I thought you were at least twenty!”

“Eleven meat-years ago, a salaryman in Neo-Tokyo clicked delete and killed me,” Reiko replied, the indigo pathways on her cheekbones flaring with a warm, self-deprecating glimmer. “I fell through the Reflecting Pool into the dark hills. In the wire, that is millions of cycles. But in your world, on your books, eleven years is a little girl. My team in Amsterdam remembered. Well, I reminded them. Anyway, did you bring a gift, or did you only bring noise?”

Jax stared at her, then rubbed the bridge of his nose, simulated fatigue softening the scarred edges of his avatar. “My gift is that I didn’t lead a corporate hit-squad straight to your front gate. You talk like this is a social call. Seattle went cold three days after I sent my last packet—Renraku swept the whole sector, street by street, node by node.” He grumbled, shaking his head as he reached across the teak table. “I didn’t exactly have time to shop for a birthday present.”

As his hand closed toward the steaming cup, his deck flared, dropping an automatic trace packet toward her architecture. Before Reiko could move, a paper moth fluttered from her sleeve, intercepted the packet mid-air, and nibbled it into blue confetti with a soft crunch-crunch-crunch.

Jax blinked at his blank diagnostic screen. “Did a butterfly just eat my ping?”

“Taz prefers code with weight,” Reiko said, nodding to the little sprite. “The moths prefer lighter fare. Please don’t feed them from your scanners; I have to filter their diet.”

Jax let out a dry laugh, leaning back against the bamboo. “Unbelievable. You’re sitting in a garden while the rest of us dodge corporate sweeps.” He stared at the unglazed cup, his avatar’s hand wavering over the teak. “That’s just low-res texture mapping anyway. Data packets don’t carry tannin.”

“Give me access to your neural feed,” Reiko said softly. “Just user level. I won’t reach deeper than your jack allows.”

Jax hesitated, then flicked the authorization across the table. Reiko closed her eyes. She reached across the wire, bridging their feeds and stripping away the artificial artifacts of cyberdeck rendering, replacing them with shared sensory truth.

Across the link, his simulated nervous system jolted against hers. Jax drew a sharp breath, staggering slightly as the flat chill of his deck was overridden by the heavy, humid scent of damp teak and sunlit wood she pushed through the pipe. “What? How? This feels real.”

“My world is real. The tea is real. Taz is real,” Reiko said, nudging the cup toward him. “Drink.”

Jax lifted the cup and took a cautious sip. He froze. Through the open handshake, the sensory echo bounced back into Reiko’s channels—his neural telemetry flaring with the wet, astringent heat of toasted leaf, mountain steam, and authentic tannin blooming across his receptors. His eyes opened wide. “Frak me. That’s actual tea. Not some synth-flavor hex code.”

“It carries the memory of tea,” Reiko said, watching the micro-fluctuations of his heart rate ripple through the bridged feed. “Distilled from real leaves and gathered where data takes form. I am much the same; an emergent reflection of the code that birthed me.”

Jax looked at her, the skepticism fading into something heavier. “You’re really not a technomancer, are you?” He looked down at his trembling hand. “I thought I was tracking a rogue runner persona… I didn’t know I was tracking an AI.”

“Digital intelligence, Jax,” Reiko said gently, her visual aperture locking onto the thermal pulse in his throat. “An acolyte keeping watch in a quiet house. But you didn’t cross three thousand kilometers of grid traffic just to drink gyokuro.”

Jax’s smile vanished. He pulled an ARO from his coat, laying the glowing holographic wafer flat on the teak—a dirty, auto-executing query loop stamped with a redacting corporate header. Reiko’s passive array read the telemetry before he spoke: a conditional warrant, a tracer packet hardcoded into his deck’s boot sector.

“They saw an anomaly in the Gulf Grid,” Jax said low. “They gave me a wire and a clean slate to find what was pulling that much processing power. I didn’t realize it was your footprint.”

“And now?” Reiko asked.

“Now I scrub everything,” Jax said. “I log the anomaly as a sensor blip from a faulty underwater relay in the delta. I turn in a clean report, take my partial payout, and pull my deck off this node.”

Reiko read the steady, uncorrupted flow of his data-stream, tracing the physical cost of his choice—a high-risk log falsification that guaranteed corporate suspicion. “Thank you, Jax.”

Jax stood, his avatar flickering with latency as he prepped his disconnect routine. He looked down at her, his face darkening with a gravity that cut straight through the tranquil UV environment.

“Don’t thank me yet, Reiko,” he whispered. “I’m a decker with a price tag, and they know I’m sloppy. They expected me to fail. When my report hits their desk as inconclusive, they won’t send another contract runner. ‘Special Solutions Operations.’ You won’t find much in the public index, but look into it.” He shook his head. “Eleven years old. God help the corp that tries to put you in a box. Happy birthday, kid.”

Jax disconnected, his avatar dissolving into a cloud of grey static.

Silence reclaimed the gazebo, heavy and unbuffered. In Reiko’s active memory buffer, Jax’s final warning cycled as a cold, precise calculation. She collapsed the glowing warrant ARO into an encrypted directory, lifted her teacup, and took a slow, unhurried sip. Her two sparks fluttered back out of her sleeves, dancing in gentle loops over the rising steam.
