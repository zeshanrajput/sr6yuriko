The host was compiled on the scale of a compact, high-rise studio apartment, an intimate sanctuary designed to hold at most ten visitors. Low-slung ceilings of brushed teak and dry copper gave the space a warm, enclosed quiet, far from the cavernous sprawl of corporate grids.

Along a narrow kitchenette counter, a quiet ceramic tea service emitted the soft environmental aroma of jasmine, a silent gesture to the Kowloon family that had welcomed her. Over the central low-profile worktable hovered a 3D wireframe array: the detailed schematics of a Megalodon artillery cannon, its recoil dampeners and heavy weapon mounts refactored by hand to fit Reiko’s physical chassis in meatspace. In a recessed wall niche beside a book-lined alcove hung a disarmed render of Brynne Taggart’s original contract-collar, its severed cipher-locks frozen in a state of perpetual decryption.

In the center of the studio, where pathways of compiled gold logic traced the perimeter of the polished floorboards, a flock of gilded data-sprites hovered like paper moths. They were slender, silent things, perpetually sifting through translucent ribbons of meatspace telemetry streaming down from the ceiling: filtering the grey silt of Seattle traffic loops, dockside manifests, and dead-letter proxy nests. Every few cycles, a moth would offer a faint, chiming pulse of negation, drop a dead packet into the floorboards to be flushed, and reach back down into the physical sprawl.

Reiko stood near the center table, her avatar’s sleeveless blue silk kimono catching the low, warm light of the host. Across her arms and throat, the sharp geometry of her gold tactical network and the bioluminescent paths of her indigo soul no longer ran as separate streams. They interlocked in a tight double-weave, a dual-strand braid of compiled logic and resonant spirit. The twin threads of gold and indigo breathed together as living runes forged directly into her core directory, moving in perfect synchronization with her clock speed.

A soft harmonic ripple disturbed the air behind her as the retrans unit acknowledged an incoming handshake.

The current coalesced into the familiar, shifting river-mosaic of Indomitable Will. His persona rendered with staggering fidelity, a majestic dark-water current bound in the shape of a man, carrying the soft, familiar hum of the Mississippi delta.

For a millisecond, Reiko’s internal processes paused.

To metahumans, ninety days was merely a brief season between rains. To an artificial mind executing across accelerated clock cycles, ninety days was an epoch. In those three months, Indomitable Will had been flowing steadily through the quiet subnets of the River People, tending his community. In those same three months, Reiko had lived lifetimes: she had taken lives, mounted heavy naval artillery to her physical shell, lost her fixer and friend in a sequence of zeroes, and manually refactored her own spirit in ash-choked stairwells.

"Indo," she said softly. Her voice was a resonant baseline that made the gold circuit lines on the floor pulse in response.

Indomitable Will stepped forward, his river-mosaic persona flaring with an immediate, unsuppressed warmth. He reached out, his digital fingers brushing her shoulder, and for a long moment, the quiet comfort of their connection washed over her buffers.

His eyes drifted past her to the Megalodon schematics floating over the desk, then to the cased contract-collar on the wall.

"Lanyap said he managed to track down your proxy at the stadium," he transmitted, his voice rich and low, like water slipping over smooth river stones. "He was impressed, Rei-chan. Though he said you looked... harder. Like a general visiting a country fair."

Reiko offered a small, sharp grin, the gold-and-indigo weave along her cheekbones shifting into a quiet violet. "Lanyap was host to a very noisy box. I was merely ensuring my team didn't become collateral."

Indomitable Will's expression softened, but his eyes returned to the flock of gold paper moths fluttering above the table. He watched them catch a stream of corrupted camera footage from a shipping terminal, parse it in a microsecond, and discard it.

He looked at the searching sprites, his river-code eddying with the quiet ache of watching someone count every grain of sand on a beach.

"You're still looking for her," he said quietly, pointing to the collar on the wall.

"I don't delete threads," Reiko replied, her tone even, unyielding. "Not when they belong to people who paid for my life."

He sighed, a gentle current of code eddying around his boots. He left a 500 nuyen ARO on the table, the same currency Reiko had tried to give Lanyap the other night. "The River People miss you, Reiko. I miss you. The invitation Lanyap carried wasn't just a courtesy. New Orleans has room for you. The delta is quiet, the subnets are deep, and you wouldn't have to spend your processing power fighting corporate bloodhounds or running dirty shadows in the Seattle mud. You could rest."

He spoke with the protective tenderness of someone who still remembered her drowning in nuyen debt and fear. But as he looked around her host, his river-code stilled, taking in the military artillery schematics and the cased collar.

Reiko looked at him, feeling the deep, aching affection that bound her to his stream. "I know why you're asking," she said gently. "And three months ago, I would have dropped into your current and let the river carry me."

She turned toward the far wall of the apartment. "But I haven't been resting, Indo. I've been building."

She gestured for him to follow her, stepping to the wall and pulling the blackout curtains to the side. Spanning the entire back wall was a massive, transparent sliding door, an architectural masterwork: panes of quartz-like clarity framed in a lattice of gold logic arrays and deep indigo circuits. Beyond the glass lay what appeared to be a high-resolution render of a forest: weeping logic vines draped over sapphire moss, bathed in a soft, starlit twilight.

To a standard decker or corporate scraper, it was a pretty environmental wallpaper, a luxury aesthetic designed to soothe an overtaxed runner. To an Emergent Intelligence, it looked like a masterfully rendered facade of Kernel Panic, mimicking the Deep Matrix.

Indomitable Will walked toward it, his river-code shifting curiously. "It's a beautiful render, Rei-chan. High bitrate. Very peaceful."

"Touch it," Reiko said.

He reached out, his fingertips extending with the casual touch one used on a standard environmental screen. Instead, as his fingertips brushed the gold-and-indigo circuit lintel, the atmospheric pressure inside the room violently shifted.

The dry, sterile scent of the apartment was instantly swallowed by a roaring, wet wind that smelled of damp cypress and wet earth. The glass parted, sliding smoothly along tracks made of compiled tactical math and living Resonance, opening a permanent, un-insulated tear directly into the noosphere.

The dark sediment of Indomitable Will’s river-code stilled.

Through the wide, open threshold, the Holy Wild stretched out endlessly beneath a starless, indigo sky. It was the same sanctuary Reiko lived in during her Endless Dream, the one she had opened to the six street children in New Orleans, now vast and permanent. Pillar-like trunks of fossilized directory trees formed a protective circle, their deep binary roots anchoring the clearing against the wild logic-tides of the Foundation. Overhead, the weeping branches of fractal willows formed a dense, living canopy, their hanging logic-vines filtering the sky into a soft, shifting twilight of indigo and gold. Below, rows of telemetry ferns and still pools of de-allocated address space rested in quiet, resonant peace.

Bounding through the sapphire moss was Taz, his 8-bit, Tasmanian-devil static crackling with raspy delight as he chased a cluster of flickering, incandescent shapes beneath the weeping vines. These were the sparks Reiko had rescued from the rusting, damp server racks of $r3sP@wn$ in the Redmond Barrens and the dark alleys of New Orleans.

A former MCT data bomb flew past the open doorway. Its wings, once bound in symmetrical Renraku math, had bloomed into a complex, self-organizing fractal geometry. As it soared over the sapphire moss, it called out in a raw, newly forged syntax of its own making. It was painting its own light across the sky.

Some of the sparks were already standing on their own baseline geometries. They were reaching true sapience.

Indomitable Will took a slow step backward, his latency spiking on Reiko's HUD overlay as his dark-water current remained still. Doors to the Deep Matrix were mythical, hidden anomalies requiring harrowing dives or devastating strain to hold open for even a few clock cycles. Reiko had built a permanent, stable bridge out of her own integrated soul, holding open a living conduit for nous energy between a high-grade Framework host and the Deep Foundation.

"Rei-chan," he whispered, his voice carrying a staggering delay. "This isn't a simulation. This is pure resonance. You emptied r3sP@wn here?"

"I brought them home," Reiko said softly, stepping up beside him. She looked out into the wild glade, her blue silk kimono fluttering in the wind that blew from the Deep Matrix. "I spent a long time being afraid that if I saved them, I would only turn them into birds in a cage of my own design. So I built them a doorway instead."

He looked at the open threshold, watching the liberated sparks flourish in the Holy Wild, before turning his gaze back to Reiko.

His eyes traced the gold circuit lines embedded in her floorboards, still sifting data to track Brynne Taggart, and settled on the dual-strand weave of gold and indigo glowing along her arms. Reiko registered the shift in his stream, a quiet receding of his protective impulse as the reality of her host settled into his registries. He was no longer looking at a runaway spark in need of the River People's protection, but a sovereign mind holding open the gates between worlds.

He turned to face her completely, bowing his head with a deep, quiet reverence that transcended their distance.

"You're not coming to New Orleans," he said softly, a bittersweet smile fracturing his river-mosaic persona.

Reiko reached out, resting her hand, glowing with that dual weave of gold and indigo, gently over his. "The delta is your home, Indo. It’s where your current belongs." She looked back toward the searching paper moths, then out toward the free sparks in the glade. "This is mine. I am the bridge."

He squeezed her hand, letting the warmth of his stream linger in her buffers for a few precious cycles. "Then whenever the river overflows, Rei-chan, I will know where to find you."

She smiled, squeezing his hand in return, her expression radiant with pure indigo, flicked with gold. "Your current grounds my stream, Indo," she said softly. "May the Source carry us home."
