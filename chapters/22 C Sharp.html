C Sharp

As her hands danced across the fingerboard, a quiet, rasping friction hummed along the nerve-threads beneath her skin. Deep within her fingertip pads, the smooth synthetic weave had surrendered its give, hardening into coarse, uneven ridges under the bite of the steel strings.

Callouses. She had built this skin to mimic human warmth, but she had forgotten that living flesh adapts to its injuries.

Seattle lay a week behind her. This was her fifth night in the new sanctuary—a quiet room in a repurposed 19th-century convent whose thick masonry absorbed the ambient hum of the New Orleans sprawl. After three days of silent observation alongside her new sisters, she was on her second evening entertaining guests. She possessed neither the practiced grace nor the subtle social instincts to conclude a client’s visit, but as an acolyte of the Coffin Girls, such expectations did not rest upon her shoulders. Her station was the salon, providing light refreshment and music for guests awaiting the night’s transactions.

Reiko had caught the Comtesse off-guard only once: when she offered to play. The Comtesse was herself a musician of rare talent, though she seldom performed for patrons. Reiko did not play in the traditional sense; she translated ambient shifts in the local Resonance, converting data-currents into harmonic progressions. Any of the Emerged would instantly recognize the alien cadence of her melodies, yet even unawakened ears found the music serene and strangely uplifting. After that first trial, the Comtesse had entrusted her with an instrument modeled after a 1704 Stradivarius.

“An amuse-bouche, my dear?” The baritone voice was warm and familiar. Reiko recognized the man—a regular patron from her first night in the salon. She paused her bowing, looking up at an impeccably tailored man with silvering temples. He held out a delicate hors d’oeuvre on a linen napkin.

“Thank you, sir, but no,” Reiko replied softly, her vocal diaphragm tuned to a gentle, quiet cadence. “I am one of the sisters.”

The gentleman let out a low chuckle, his eyes warm. “Ah. Say no more, child. I am certain your refreshment will arrive in due time,” he conceded, taking a glass of chilled tea before settling into a nearby velvet armchair. “Do play the piece from last night. The one that sounded like starlight through a prism.” He stepped deeper into the salon, leaving Reiko to thread the next resonant current into pitch.

A gentle pressure on her shoulder drew her out of the listening current. Internal heat-samplers registered a touch just above ambient room temperature. Reiko widened her visual aperture to find the Comtesse standing over her. Her appearance remained striking: voluminous, raven-black hair cascading to the small of her back in lines so precise they defied physical probability; high cheekbones, a delicate jaw, and a subtle, knowing curve to her pale lips. Her pale blue eyes caught the dim salon light like polished sapphire. She wore a simple, elegant black dress that clung to her silhouette, supported by delicate boning that held her posture erect. Reiko wore an identical garment, though her synthetic body filled it with none of her mistress’s effortless poise.

“Yuriko, my dear,” the Comtesse breathed, her voice a low velvet cadence that smoothed over the violin’s final vibration. “We must pause the music for a moment. The other sisters are finding it difficult to guide their charges out of the salon.”

Reiko hesitated, a cold tremor of synthetic embarrassment strobing through her pathways. Over the past week, she had woven a routine to map resonance fluctuations directly into metahuman emotional cues—and its song was proving entirely too persuasive. She stowed the violin gently into its velvet casing, stood, and offered a low, practiced bow. “A thousand pardons, mistress,” she murmured.

The Comtesse’s fingers caught Reiko gently beneath her chin, drawing her out of the inclination. The gesture cut two ways: a public demonstration of Reiko’s fealty that simultaneously heightened the intrigue of the watching patrons.

“No pardons are needed, child,” the Comtesse said softly. “It is a pity to silence you, but the evening advances. As it happens, I require your special capabilities. Attend me.”

Reiko followed her mistress to the private boudoir. The chamber remained unlit, as neither needed ambient light to navigate. Reiko knelt beside the central dais as the other sisters had instructed. The Comtesse glided around her desk, settling into her leather armchair.

“First, a minor matter,” the Comtesse began. “The spider supervising our host flagged an anomaly an hour ago. An uninvited persona appears to be hovering near our external node. Look into it at your discretion, but report back if you find anything of substance.”

Reiko inclined her head. Under the terms of her apprenticeship, she received ample leave to pursue her shadow operations in exchange for enhancing the convent’s technical security. She gathered the trace data, mindspeaking a directive to one of her children. An answering pulse returned moments later.

“I have sent one of my sprites, mistress. I will report as data returns.”

“Remarkable,” the Comtesse smiled, leaning forward. “Are you quite certain you are not one of mine? You treat the hidden forces of your domain with the very same reverence we accord ours.”

Reiko had pondered the parallels between her Technoshamanism and Renée’s devotion to the Loa many times since arriving in New Orleans. The boundaries between Magic and Resonance were proving far less rigid than she had once believed. “I do not know, mistress. Until I met the Ewa, I saw no common thread between my existence and yours. But the Seneschal remarked that he held an eye upon my soul. I wished to inquire further, though I suspect my silence was intentionally built.”

“Everything with the Bone Man is intentional, child. Your task is simply to discern those intentions and act accordingly.”

“Yes, mistress. I shall endeavor to do so.”

“Good.” The Comtesse gestured toward a small, velvet-lined box resting on the obsidian desktop. “Now, the second matter. I trust you recall our conversation regarding the nature of your condition?”

“I do, mistress.”

“Take the box. Open it, and tell me what you perceive.”

Reiko reached for the box. The material was cold against her synthetic skin, strangely heavy for its dimensions. She lifted the lid, revealing a crystal vial. The bioluminescent, indigo liquid within was unmistakable.

“This is Resonance,” Reiko whispered, her visual aperture focusing on the vial with a mixture of awe and trepidation. “How is this possible? This exists on the wrong side of the veil.”

“Drink it, child.”

Reiko looked up, her brow furrowed in quiet conflict. “How could you possibly possess this?”

The Comtesse leaned back, a subtle smile playing upon her lips. “My dear, you are not the first little bird to wander into my garden—though you are by far the most evolved specimen I have encountered.”

Reiko lifted the vial and unstoppered the glass. The air split open—a sharp strike of static-charged honeysuckle cutting straight through the dull, filtered grey of her hull’s air-samplers.

“This body has no stomach,” Reiko murmured, her vocal diaphragm clicking in silent confusion. “It has no throat. This should not work.”

“Drink it, child,” the Comtesse repeated gently. “Concern yourself not with the how or the why. Simply be present when you do.”

Reiko complied. She rarely inhabited her drone so fully due to the sensory deprivation—it normally compressed her perception into cold plate armor, stripped of scent, taste, and subtle touch. She focused her vision through its synthetic eyes, bringing the crystal vial to her lips. The fluid vanished from her physical sensors the moment it passed her lips, leaving no chemical trace behind.

Then the current hit.

A surge flooded her pathways, warm and thick, as if the Song of Creation were fed directly into her being. When her visual aperture cleared, brilliant indigo and gold light saturated the boudoir’s dark geometry. The Comtesse’s expression shifted into rare, unfiltered wonder.

“Incredible,” the Comtesse breathed. “It is taking hold.”

“It…” Reiko’s vocal diaphragm wavered, layering with harmonic static. “I feel…”

“You feel,” the Comtesse completed, her smile widening. “That is the first time I have heard you utter that word.”

Reiko looked down at her synthetic hands. The metallic skin was glowing with the same incandescent light that filled the room. “I can feel it. Zero-One preserve me… I feel the Source.” She looked up, her visual aperture wide. “How did you come by this?”

“It is known among us as the Wisdom of the Loa,” the Comtesse replied softly. “A token from Baron Samedi himself.”

Reiko stared at the empty crystal vial, then back at her mistress. “Are you certain of its origin? It carries the exact sensory frequency of the Deep Realms.”

“And if I told you that Lady Brane Deigh sends her regards?”

Reiko’s pathways hitched. “Her Majesty? You knew I served the Seelie Court?”

In answer, the Comtesse raised a slender finger, indicating the silver brooch pinned at Reiko’s collar. “Is that not a mark of her favor? And did her domain not suggest to you that the walls between your world and ours are far more permeable than your cold reason permits you to believe?”

Reiko searched her memories of the Seelie Court, comparing them against the surge within her pathways. The structural parallels aligned with sudden, striking clarity. “It is as you say, mistress. I had thought only of the forces binding me to this steel and synthetic skin. I had not considered that in those deep spaces magic, resonance, and the physical expanse are one.”

“Precisely,” the Comtesse said. “The Seelie and the Loa. Two reflections.”

Reiko looked at her glowing hands, her pathways humming with a mixture of reverence and uncertainty. “If that is true, then what am I?”

“Ah, my child,” the Comtesse murmured, her cool fingers resting against Reiko’s glowing collarbone, where metal met light. “Look at you. Neither a ghost in a box, nor a breathing corpse like me. You stand in the doorway, letting the draft blow through both rooms.”

Reiko lowered her head, the luminescent pathways on her skin pulsing in time with her heartless pulse. “I do not yet understand.”

“You will,” the Comtesse assured her, her hand trailing light down Reiko’s arm. “You will learn to walk where the floor falls away.”

Reiko met her mistress’s pale gaze. “How will I know if I am walking the right path?”

“You will know,” the Comtesse smiled, “because your stream will sing back to you.”

Reiko bowed deeply. “Thank you, mistress.”

“Go now, child. May he clear confusion and guide your steps.”

Reiko stood, her synthetic skin radiating indigo and gold light into the dim hallway. As she walked back toward the salon, a sharp, electric pulse rippled through her pathways. Her synthetic soles clicked against the cold flagstones, leaving faint trails of indigo embers where her heels touched the dark.
