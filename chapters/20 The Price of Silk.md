# The Price of Silk

<audio controls src="audio/20%20The%20Price%20of%20Silk.mp3" style="width: 100%; margin-bottom: 20px;"></audio>

In a narrow, covered arcade where rows of red lanterns cast long, trembling reflections across damp concrete, Reiko walked with an unhurried, hydraulic grace. Her chassis moved silently behind the elderly grandmother who had opened her apartment door three days ago to a nervous, Mandarin-speaking investigator.

The grandmother, whose name the tactical network had cataloged simply as *Elder_Host_3B*, tapped her cane rhythmically against the stone paving. Every few steps, she glanced back to ensure the petite, porcelain-skinned girl with the dark, straight bangs was keeping pace.

"My daughter has a stall near the old water conduit," the old woman said, her voice crinkling at the corners with affectionate pride. "She thinks she is the only seamstress in the district who knows how to pleat silk without letting the humidity ruin the warp. I told her about you."

Reiko’s social HUD quietly logged the classification. [SUBJECT: ELDER_KINDNESS // RECOGNIZED INFLUENCE: POSITIVE]. Along her throat and wrists, the bioluminescent neon-indigo pathways flared with a soft, warm luminescence, mapping an authentic emotional resonance that required no tactical override.

"I only asked directions, Grandmother," Reiko replied in soft, low-cadence Cantonese.

The old woman let out a dry, rattling laugh. "And you lingered. Kindness is a rare commodity in this ward, child. Come."

They turned a corner into a stall draped from floor to ceiling in bolts of dark fabric, heavy brocades, synthetic meshes, and deep, light-absorbing silks. Behind a large wooden cutting table cluttered with spools of red and gold thread stood a woman in her late forties with sharp, intelligent eyes and a measuring tape slung casually around her neck like a stethoscope.

"Ah-Mei," the grandmother announced, waving a hand toward Reiko. "This is the one I told you about. The lovely child who checked up on me the other day."

Ah-Mei looked up from a half-finished hem, her gaze taking in Reiko’s porcelain face, the smooth sweep of her Shiawase collar, and the subtle, breathing pulse of the indigo circuits beneath her skin. A knowing, amused curve touched the seamstress's lips.

"The one who looked through my mother's window as if she were inspecting a military bunker?" Ah-Mei asked, though her eyes were kind. She set down her shears. "My mother has talked of nothing else. Sit, child. Or stand, if your joints prefer it."

"This body requires no rest," Reiko murmured politely, stepping further into the stall.

As she moved, her optical sensors swept across the racks, cataloging the inventory. And there, hanging near the back corner where the lantern light caught its dark weave, was the qipao.

It was a modern, form-fitting black halter-neck dress, lined with subtle, traditional floral embroidery that traced the seams like faint golden code. Its back cut was low and clean. The TacNet projected it would precisely clear the thermal-dissipation vents of her carbon-fiber wing arrays without snagging the silk.

Reiko froze. Her internal gold logic core flared, running a rapid spatial-geometry recheck. *Vector analysis: optimal alignment. Clearance parameters: verified.* Ah-Mei caught the direction of her gaze. A small smile played at the seamstress's mouth. She walked over, unhooked the hanger from the rack, and held the garment out toward Reiko.

"My mother saw you admire a cheongsam when she saw you the other day," Ah-Mei said, her tone light but unyielding. "Take it."

Reiko’s hands, fine-manipulation tools designed for delicate cyber-splicing and weapon maintenance, extended tentatively. Her fingers brushed the cool, heavy silk. It was exquisite. Immediately, a rigid, unyielding subroutine tripped inside her core directory.

*Transaction Protocol Error.* In the shadows, nothing came without a cost. Every favor carried interest; every asset demanded a ledger entry. Even her safehouse in the Barrens had been chained to Brynne’s crimson-bordered debt contract, until Reiko had lost her.

"I cannot take this without compensation," Reiko said, her voice steady, her Cantonese cadence dropping into a precise, literal translation of fair exchange. She reached into her pocket and produced a crisp, unblemished nuyen credstick, placing it carefully on the wooden cutting table beside a spool of red thread. "The material is high-density silk. The labor is skilled. The market value requires an equal transfer of value."

Ah-Mei stared at the credstick, then looked up at Reiko’s face. The seamstress crossed her arms, a look of mock sternness settling over her features.

"Compensation?" Ah-Mei scoffed gently. "You walked my mother home when the Triad patrols were breathing down the stairwells. You spoke to her with respect when the corporate suits wouldn't even look her in the eye. That isn't a commodity you buy with a plastic stick, girl."

"The transaction is unbalanced," Reiko insisted, her gold tactical lines flickering across her temples as her TacNet struggled to parse the missing variable. "Standard market etiquette dictates a negotiation. If the price is too high, I am to offer a lower percentage. If the price is zero, the system logic collapses."

Ah-Mei burst out laughing, a warm, resonant sound that echoed against the narrow alley walls. The elderly grandmother chuckled beside her, leaning heavily on her cane.

"Listen to her," the grandmother beamed, patting Reiko’s ceramic arm. "She talks like a little bank clerk."

"I am not a clerk," Reiko murmured, though the indigo circuits on her cheeks flushed with a soft, self-deprecating warmth. "I am learning to participate correctly."

"There is no correct calculation for a gift, little one," Ah-Mei said, stepping closer. She picked up the black qipao and deliberately draped it over Reiko’s arm, folding the cool silk against the charcoal ceramic plates. She pushed the credstick back across the wood, sliding it right into Reiko’s palm and closing the metal fingers over it. "Consider it paid in full by the peace of mind you gave an old woman. Now take it before I change my mind and charge you double for arguing with a seamstress."

Reiko stood motionless for three full seconds, her internal clock-speed cycling through a dozen linguistic and cultural subroutines. Every human market vector she had ever mapped in the sprawl assumed hostility, power imbalances, or debt. This, an unearned garment given simply out of human grace and neighborhood kinship, did not fit any equation in her database.

Slowly, her fingers tightened around the hanger. The neon-indigo pathways on her skin flared with a deep, radiant luminescence, completely unmasking her emotional core, while her gold logic circuits quietly stepped down to a resting pulse.

She bowed her head with a deep, deliberate grace that set aside the rigid mechanics of a corporate drone.

"Thank you, Ah-Mei," Reiko whispered, her voice carrying a quiet, resonant harmony that made the nearby hanging lanterns shimmer. "I will endeavor to be worthy of this gift."

As she turned to leave the stall, she held the black silk against her shoulder like a badge of an entirely different kind of citizenship.
